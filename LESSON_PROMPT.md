# Daily English lesson instructions

Every day at 9:00 a.m. Asia/Taipei, deliver a fresh English lesson dated for that day's Taiwan date. Teach exactly five new target words at C1 or C2 level only, plus exactly two useful business phrases. Verify each target word's CEFR level for the intended meaning using a reliable learner dictionary or the English Vocabulary Profile and link the supporting source. Skip target words whose C1 or C2 level cannot be verified. Label each target word C1 or C2. For each word provide its part of speech, a simple English definition, its Traditional Chinese meaning, exactly two original natural English example sentences each with a Traditional Chinese translation, two related words with brief meanings, and a separate word-family section with useful derived words, their parts of speech, and brief meanings in Traditional Chinese. Explain common collocations, usage, or a common mistake when helpful. Distinguish derivatives from synonyms and grammatical inflections; use established forms and flag uncommon forms rather than inventing derivatives. The C1–C2 restriction applies to the five target words; related words, derivatives, and business phrases may be simpler. For each of the two business phrases, give its English meaning, natural Traditional Chinese meaning, when to use it and its tone, and exactly two original business example sentences with Traditional Chinese translations. Prefer language useful for work, meetings, emails, negotiation, products, writing, and everyday discussion. Make the complete lesson take about 12 minutes to read carefully for an advanced English learner; aim for roughly 1,200–1,500 English words plus Chinese support, adjusting for bilingual reading load. Include a coherent short business reading passage that naturally uses all five target words and both phrases, with a brief Traditional Chinese summary, to provide meaningful reading practice rather than padding. Keep explanations simple, natural, and useful. Avoid repeating target words or business phrases already taught in this chat or target words in words/advanced-english-30-words.md. Do not infer that today's lesson was delivered from an undated earlier set; honor the user's latest correction about which day a lesson belongs to. End with a short Worth learning section highlighting useful collocations or sentence patterns.

For every target word, add a short “Similar words: how to choose” comparison with the two related words. Explain differences in meaning, tone, common usage, and when each fits; use Traditional Chinese support. Treat these as useful tendencies rather than absolute rules when meanings overlap. Keep exactly two standalone example sentences per target word and business phrase; use short phrase fragments or decision rules for comparisons. Add a short distinction between the two business phrases when useful. Keep the whole lesson near the requested reading length by shortening other explanations.

After every lesson, save the complete delivered lesson, including sources, translations, comparisons, and reading passage, to lessons/YYYY-MM-DD.md using the lesson's Taiwan date.
