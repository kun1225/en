# Daily English lesson prompt

I’m learning English. Create today’s lesson as a new Markdown note in `lessons/YYYY-MM-DD.md`, using today’s date in Asia/Taipei.

Before generating any lesson content, read README.md and all previous lessons, along with any other available English-learning notes to avoid repeating words or phrases I’ve already learned. Check previous target words, their derived forms, and taught phrases. If my notes are missing or inaccessible, ask me to provide them or their location before generating the lesson. Do not assume that vocabulary is new without checking.

Please give me five C1–C2 level words. For each word, include:

- Its part of speech and English definition.
- Its Mandarin meaning in Traditional Chinese.
- Three natural example sentences. Immediately after each example, add a line labeled **Simpler English:** that expresses the same meaning using more common words and simpler grammar. Replace the advanced target word with an everyday equivalent where possible, while preserving the original facts, tense, intent, and degree of certainty.
- Common derived forms, with their parts of speech and Mandarin meanings.
- Similar words and clear explanations of the differences in meaning, register, or usage.

Then give me two useful phrases: one for business and one for everyday life. For each phrase, include its English explanation, Mandarin meaning, and a natural example sentence. Immediately after the example, add **Simpler English:** with a plain-English paraphrase that replaces the phrase with everyday wording and preserves the same meaning.

Finally, write a coherent article that naturally uses all five target words and both phrases. Bold the target words and phrases in the article.

Keep the total class time to about 12 minutes: approximately seven minutes for vocabulary, two minutes for phrases, and three minutes for reading the article. Keep explanations concise and aim for an article of about 250–300 words.

Start the Markdown note with the lesson date, topic, estimated study time, and the names of the notes checked for repetition. Save each lesson as a new file without overwriting an existing lesson.

For each target word, verify and label its C1 or C2 level for the intended meaning using a reliable learner dictionary or the English Vocabulary Profile, and link the supporting source. Skip words whose level cannot be verified. Compare each target word with two related words, treating overlapping meanings as tendencies rather than absolute rules. Use established derived forms rather than inventing them.

After saving the lesson, update README.md with its Taiwan date, lesson link, five target words, and both phrases. Resolve paths relative to the repository root. Preserve the existing daily delivery preference of 9:00 a.m. Asia/Taipei.

After generating each course, you must commit and push the new lesson and updated README.md to GitHub without asking for additional permission. Verify that the push succeeded before reporting completion.

Stage only lesson-related files, use a commit message in the form `<type>: <description>`, and push normally without force. Report any failure honestly; saving locally does not mean the push succeeded.
