# 30 Advanced English Words

Study five words a day for six days. These are advanced study selections, not certified word-by-word CEFR ratings. CEFR ends at C2; D1 is not a CEFR level.

Read each example aloud, then write one sentence about your own life.

## Day 1: Meaning and arguments

### 1. nuance (noun)

**Meaning:** A small but important difference in meaning or feeling.

1. The translation misses the nuance of the original joke.
2. A good manager understands the nuances of team communication.

**Word family:** nuanced (adjective): showing fine differences in meaning or judgment.

**Related words:** subtlety: a fine detail; distinction: a difference.

### 2. cogent (adjective)

**Meaning:** Clear, logical, and convincing.

1. She made a cogent argument for delaying the launch.
2. The report gives a cogent explanation of the failure.

**Word family:** cogency (noun): the quality of being clear and convincing; cogently (adverb): in a clear, convincing way.

**Related words:** coherent: logically connected; persuasive: able to convince.

### 3. tenable (adjective)

**Meaning:** Able to be defended with good reasons.

1. That position is no longer tenable given the new evidence.
2. We need a tenable explanation for the drop in sales.

**Word family:** tenability (noun): the ability to be defended; untenable (adjective): impossible to defend.

**Related words:** defensible: supported by good reasons; untenable: impossible to defend.

### 4. equivocal (adjective)

**Meaning:** Unclear or open to more than one interpretation.

1. His equivocal answer left us unsure whether he agreed.
2. The evidence is equivocal, so we need more research.

**Word family:** equivocally (adverb): in an unclear or ambiguous way; unequivocal (adjective): completely clear.

**Related words:** ambiguous: having multiple possible meanings; unequivocal: completely clear.

### 5. salient (adjective)

**Meaning:** Most noticeable or important.

1. She summarized the salient points in one paragraph.
2. Cost is the most salient issue for small teams.

**Word family:** salience (noun): the quality of standing out or being important.

**Related words:** prominent: easily noticed; relevance: connection to the issue.

## Day 2: Judgment and care

### 6. pragmatic (adjective)

**Meaning:** Focused on what works in practice.

1. We took a pragmatic approach and fixed the biggest problem first.
2. Her pragmatic advice helped us finish on time.

**Word family:** pragmatism (noun): a focus on workable solutions; pragmatically (adverb): in a practical way.

**Related words:** practical: useful in real situations; pragmatism: focus on workable solutions.

### 7. judicious (adjective)

**Meaning:** Showing careful thought and good judgment.

1. Judicious use of animation can make an interface easier to understand.
2. A judicious investment in training saved us time later.

**Word family:** judiciously (adverb): with careful judgment; judiciousness (noun): good judgment.

**Related words:** prudent: careful about risks; discretion: good judgment about how to act.

### 8. circumspect (adjective)

**Meaning:** Careful to consider risks before speaking or acting.

1. She was circumspect about sharing customer data.
2. Investors became more circumspect after the failed launch.

**Word family:** circumspection (noun): careful consideration of risks; circumspectly (adverb): cautiously.

**Related words:** cautious: careful to avoid danger; circumspection: careful consideration.

### 9. discerning (adjective)

**Meaning:** Good at noticing quality and making careful choices.

1. Discerning readers will notice the weak evidence.
2. The shop attracts discerning customers who value good design.

**Word family:** discern (verb): to notice or recognize; discernment (noun): the ability to judge well.

**Related words:** discern: notice or recognize; perceptive: quick to understand.

### 10. scrupulous (adjective)

**Meaning:** Very careful to act honestly or follow standards.

1. She is scrupulous about crediting other writers.
2. The team kept scrupulous records of every payment.

**Word family:** scrupulously (adverb): with great care or honesty; scrupulousness (noun): careful attention to standards.

**Related words:** meticulous: extremely careful about details; integrity: honesty and strong principles.

## Day 3: Evidence and explanation

### 11. substantiate (verb)

**Meaning:** To support a claim with evidence.

1. Can you substantiate that claim with customer interviews?
2. The study failed to substantiate the proposed link.

**Word family:** substantiation (noun): evidence supporting a claim; unsubstantiated (adjective): not supported by evidence.

**Related words:** corroborate: support with further evidence; substantiation: evidence supporting a claim.

### 12. corroborate (verb)

**Meaning:** To provide evidence that supports another account or finding.

1. The server logs corroborate her account of the outage.
2. Two independent studies corroborated the result.

**Word family:** corroboration (noun): additional supporting evidence; corroborative (adjective): providing supporting evidence.

**Related words:** confirm: establish that something is true; corroboration: additional supporting evidence.

### 13. refute (verb)

**Meaning:** To show that a claim is wrong using evidence or reasoning.

1. The new data refute the claim that demand is falling.
2. She refuted his argument by identifying a false assumption.

**Word family:** refutation (noun): reasoning or evidence showing a claim is wrong; refutable (adjective): able to be shown wrong.

**Related words:** rebut: argue against a claim; refutation: proof or reasoning against a claim.

### 14. elucidate (verb)

**Meaning:** To make something clearer by explaining it.

1. The diagram helps elucidate how the system works.
2. Could you elucidate the reasoning behind this decision?

**Word family:** elucidation (noun): an explanation that makes something clear.

**Related words:** clarify: make clearer; elucidation: an explanation that makes something clear.

### 15. delineate (verb)

**Meaning:** To describe or mark the limits of something clearly.

1. The document delineates each team member’s responsibilities.
2. We need to delineate the scope before estimating the cost.

**Word family:** delineation (noun): a clear description or marking of boundaries.

**Related words:** outline: describe the main points; delineation: a clear description of boundaries.

## Day 4: Difficult situations

### 16. intractable (adjective)

**Meaning:** Very difficult to solve, control, or change.

1. The team faced an intractable conflict over ownership.
2. What looked like an intractable bug had a simple cause.

**Word family:** intractability (noun): the quality of being very difficult to solve or control.

**Related words:** tractable: easy to manage; stubborn: resistant to change.

### 17. pervasive (adjective)

**Meaning:** Present or spreading throughout a place or situation.

1. A pervasive fear of failure stopped people from experimenting.
2. Smartphones have become a pervasive part of daily life.

**Word family:** pervasiveness (noun): the extent to which something is present throughout a place or situation.

**Related words:** widespread: found in many places; permeate: spread through something.

### 18. entrenched (adjective)

**Meaning:** Firmly established and difficult to change.

1. The new policy challenged entrenched habits.
2. An entrenched hierarchy made decisions slow.

**Word family:** entrench (verb): to establish firmly; entrenchment (noun): the process or state of becoming firmly established.

**Related words:** ingrained: deeply fixed as a habit; entrench: establish firmly.

### 19. precarious (adjective)

**Meaning:** Unstable or at risk of becoming worse.

1. The company was in a precarious financial position.
2. The ladder looked precarious on the uneven floor.

**Word family:** precariously (adverb): in an unstable or unsafe way; precariousness (noun): an unstable or unsafe state.

**Related words:** unstable: likely to change or fall; precariously: in an unsafe or uncertain way.

### 20. contingent (adjective)

**Meaning:** Dependent on a condition or event.

1. The launch date is contingent on the security review.
2. Our funding is contingent on meeting the agreed targets.

**Word family:** contingently (adverb): depending on a condition; contingency (noun): a possible event to prepare for.

**Related words:** conditional: subject to a requirement; contingency: a possible future event to plan for.

## Day 5: Problems and change

### 21. mitigate (verb)

**Meaning:** To make something harmful less severe.

1. We added backups to mitigate the impact of data loss.
2. Clear instructions can mitigate confusion during onboarding.

**Word family:** mitigation (noun): reduction of harm; mitigating (adjective): making something less severe.

**Related words:** alleviate: make suffering or a problem less severe; mitigation: reduction of harm.

### 22. exacerbate (verb)

**Meaning:** To make an existing problem worse.

1. Adding more features could exacerbate the performance problem.
2. Poor communication exacerbated the conflict.

**Word family:** exacerbation (noun): a worsening of a problem.

**Related words:** aggravate: make worse; exacerbation: a worsening of a problem.

### 23. reconcile (verb)

**Meaning:** To make conflicting things compatible or consistent.

1. We need to reconcile user needs with our budget.
2. She struggled to reconcile the two accounts of the event.

**Word family:** reconciliation (noun): making things compatible or restoring friendly relations; irreconcilable (adjective): impossible to make compatible.

**Related words:** resolve: settle a problem; reconciliation: making things consistent or restoring a relationship.

### 24. preclude (verb)

**Meaning:** To prevent something or make it impossible.

1. A small budget does not preclude good design.
2. The contract precludes us from sharing the source code.

**Word family:** preclusion (noun): the act of preventing or ruling something out; preclusive (adjective): preventing something.

**Related words:** prevent: stop something from happening; rule out: exclude as a possibility.

### 25. supersede (verb)

**Meaning:** To replace something older or less suitable.

1. The updated policy supersedes all previous versions.
2. Digital tickets have superseded paper tickets on this route.

**Word family:** supersession (noun): replacement by something newer; supersession is a formal, uncommon noun.

**Related words:** replace: take the place of; obsolete: no longer useful or current.

## Day 6: Time, appearances, and feelings

### 26. ephemeral (adjective)

**Meaning:** Lasting for only a short time.

1. Online attention is often ephemeral.
2. The installation uses light to create ephemeral patterns.

**Word family:** ephemerality (noun): the quality of lasting only briefly.

**Related words:** fleeting: passing very quickly; transience: the state of lasting only briefly.

### 27. ostensible (adjective)

**Meaning:** Stated or appearing to be true, though the reality may differ.

1. The ostensible purpose of the meeting was to review costs.
2. His ostensible reason for leaving was the long commute.

**Word family:** ostensibly (adverb): according to what is stated or appears true.

**Related words:** apparent: seeming to be true; ostensibly: according to what is stated or appears true.

### 28. incongruous (adjective)

**Meaning:** Strange because it does not fit its surroundings or context.

1. The playful icon looked incongruous on the serious warning screen.
2. His cheerful tone seemed incongruous during the crisis.

**Word family:** incongruity (noun): a mismatch; incongruously (adverb): in a way that does not fit the context.

**Related words:** incongruity: a mismatch; discordant: noticeably out of harmony.

### 29. ambivalent (adjective)

**Meaning:** Having conflicting feelings about something.

1. I feel ambivalent about turning my hobby into a business.
2. She was ambivalent about accepting the promotion.

**Word family:** ambivalence (noun): conflicting feelings; ambivalently (adverb): with conflicting feelings.

**Related words:** ambivalence: conflicting feelings; conflicted: pulled between opposing feelings.

### 30. prescient (adjective)

**Meaning:** Showing an ability to predict what will happen.

1. Her warning about maintenance costs proved prescient.
2. The book offers a prescient account of how technology could change work.

**Word family:** prescience (noun): the ability to foresee events; presciently (adverb): with awareness of what will happen.

**Related words:** foresight: ability to anticipate future needs or events; foresighted: planning wisely for the future.
