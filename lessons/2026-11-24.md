## Vocabulary — 7 minutes

### 1. Neoliberalism — CEFR unverified · uncountable noun

**English:** A contested term for approaches emphasizing markets, competition, and a market-oriented role for the state.  
**繁體中文：** 新自由主義；重視市場、競爭與市場導向治理的政經取向。  
**Reference:** [Dictionary reference](https://dictionary.cambridge.org/dictionary/english/neoliberalism). No C1/C2 rating verified for the intended meaning.

1. The essay examines neoliberalism in cultural policy.  
   **Simpler English:** The essay examines market-oriented ideas and policies in cultural policy.
2. The authors disagree about how to define neoliberalism.  
   **Simpler English:** The authors disagree about how to define this market-oriented political and economic approach.
3. The journal places debates about neoliberalism beside studies of public institutions.  
   **Simpler English:** The journal places debates about market-oriented governance beside studies of public institutions.

**Word family:** neoliberal (adjective/noun)：新自由主義的／支持者。  
**Compare two related words:** Liberalism is a broader family of political ideas; laissez-faire emphasizes minimal interference. Neoliberalism is not simply the absence of government; definitions differ across scholarship.

### 2. Culminate — CEFR unverified · intransitive verb

**English:** Reach a final important stage or outcome after earlier developments.  
**繁體中文：** 達到高潮；最終形成某成果。  
**Reference:** [Dictionary reference](https://dictionary.cambridge.org/dictionary/english/culminate). No C1/C2 rating verified for the intended meaning.

1. Years of research culminated in an exhibition.  
   **Simpler English:** Years of research led to a final major outcome: an exhibition.
2. The collaboration culminated in a published book.  
   **Simpler English:** The collaboration reached its final major stage with a published book.
3. The programme will culminate in a public discussion.  
   **Simpler English:** The programme will end with a major public discussion.

**Word family:** culmination (noun)：最終成果、高潮；culminating (participle/adjective)：作為最終階段的。  
**Compare two related words:** Finish simply means end; result in describes an outcome without necessarily suggesting a long buildup. Culminate often presents an outcome as the high point of preceding work.

### 3. Monograph — CEFR unverified · countable noun

**English:** A substantial work focused on one subject or, in art publishing, one artist's work.  
**繁體中文：** 專論；單一主題或藝術家的專書。  
**Reference:** [Dictionary reference](https://dictionary.cambridge.org/dictionary/english/monograph). No C1/C2 rating verified for the intended meaning.

1. The photographer's monograph brings together decades of work.  
   **Simpler English:** The photographer's book devoted to their work brings together photographs from several decades.
2. The editor helped structure the monograph.  
   **Simpler English:** The editor helped organize the book focused on one subject.
3. The monograph appeared alongside an exhibition.  
   **Simpler English:** The book focused on the artist's work was published alongside an exhibition.

**Word family:** monographic (adjective)：專論式的；monographs (plural)：多本專書。  
**Compare two related words:** Catalogue organizes or documents a collection or exhibition; anthology gathers selected works, often by multiple contributors. A monograph emphasizes one sustained focus; formats can overlap.

### 4. Sanctions — CEFR unverified · plural noun

**English:** Official restrictions or penalties intended to influence behavior, especially between states.  
**繁體中文：** 制裁；用以影響行為的官方限制或懲罰。  
**Reference:** [Dictionary reference](https://dictionary.cambridge.org/dictionary/english/sanction). No C1/C2 rating verified for the intended meaning.

1. The article examines the effects of economic sanctions.  
   **Simpler English:** The article examines the effects of official economic restrictions intended to change behavior.
2. Sanctions can affect cultural exchanges as well as trade.  
   **Simpler English:** Official restrictions can affect cultural exchanges as well as trade.
3. The authors debate whether the sanctions achieved their aims.  
   **Simpler English:** The authors debate whether the official restrictions achieved their aims.

**Word family:** sanction (noun/verb)：制裁／施加制裁；can also mean official approval in another sense.  
**Compare two related words:** Embargo often prohibits particular trade; penalty is a broader punishment. Sanctions may involve finance, travel, or trade and need not ban all exchange.

### 5. Forensic — C1 · adjective

**English:** Relating to the examination of evidence for legal or investigative purposes.  
**繁體中文：** 鑑識的；司法調查證據分析的。  
**Reference:** [Level and meaning source](https://www.oxfordlearnersdictionaries.co.uk/definition/english/forensic). The source labels the relevant meaning C1.

1. The project uses forensic analysis to examine conflicting accounts.  
   **Simpler English:** The project examines evidence closely for an investigation to assess conflicting accounts.
2. Forensic methods can help reconstruct an event.  
   **Simpler English:** Methods for examining investigative evidence can help work out how an event happened.
3. The journal discusses forensic architecture and public accountability.  
   **Simpler English:** The journal discusses investigations using architectural evidence and responsibility to the public.

**Word family:** forensics (noun)：鑑識科學；forensically (adverb)：以鑑識方式。  
**Compare two related words:** Investigative broadly concerns finding facts; architectural concerns buildings and spatial design. Forensic architecture combines spatial analysis with evidence-based investigations; not all architectural work is forensic.

**Additional expressions:** Critical theory — 批判理論：approaches examining society, power, and culture, often with an emancipatory aim; not simply being negative. Forensic architecture — 以建築及空間分析進行證據調查的領域；Forensic Architecture with capitals can name a specific organization. Culminating is a form of culminate, not a separate base verb.

## Two useful phrases — 2 minutes

### Business: Sunset a project

**English:** Plan and carry out the gradual ending of a project.  
**繁體中文：** 逐步結束或停止一個專案。  
**Example:** We will sunset the project after publishing the final issue.  
**Simpler English:** We will gradually end the project after publishing the final issue.

### Everyday: See something through

**English:** Continue working on something until it is finished.  
**繁體中文：** 把事情做到底。  
**Example:** The book took years, but she saw it through.  
**Simpler English:** The book took years, but she kept working until it was finished.

### From research to publication

*An original learning passage based on the supplied notes; explanatory observations are not additional biographical claims.*

The closing section of the supplied notes connects independent publishing with a long photographic collaboration. Lucas shared issues of Auctoritas and discussed work with Robert Bergman. These examples suggest a useful question for readers: what kinds of effort turn a research interest into a public object such as a journal, book, or exhibition?

One topic in the notes is **neoliberalism**. Because the term has several scholarly uses, a careful reader should ask how a particular author defines it. Calling something market-oriented is a starting point, not a complete explanation of its institutions or effects. A useful discussion identifies the specific policies and assumptions being examined.

The journal also addressed economic **sanctions**. Reading about them requires separating their stated goals from their observed consequences. A writer may support or criticize a policy, but the argument becomes clearer when the evidence and the judgment are distinguishable.

Another subject was **forensic** architecture. Here, spatial analysis supports investigations rather than simply designing buildings. The phrase names a field of work; it should not be taken to mean that every architectural image automatically proves a legal claim.

The notes describe a collaboration that would **culminate** in a **monograph** and an exhibition. A book focused on one photographer can require years of organizing, selecting, and editing before it reaches readers. The finished volume may conceal much of that patient work.

To **see something through** means continuing until it is complete. Equally, editors may decide to **sunset a project** after its final planned issue. Completion and continuation are different choices. A thoughtful ending can protect the value of the work already done while making room for another undertaking.

**Check your understanding:** How does a monograph differ from an anthology? Why can ending a project be a thoughtful choice?
