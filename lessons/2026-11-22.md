## Vocabulary — 7 minutes

### 1. Ethnographic — CEFR unverified · adjective

**English:** Studying people's practices and cultures through close observation.  
**繁體中文：** 民族誌的；透過深入觀察研究文化與生活實踐的。  
**Reference:** [Dictionary reference](https://dictionary.cambridge.org/dictionary/english/ethnographic). No C1/C2 rating verified for the intended meaning.

1. An ethnographic approach can reveal how archivists make everyday decisions.  
   **Simpler English:** Close observation of archivists' practices can reveal how they make everyday decisions.
2. Her ethnographic notes describe conversations as well as routines.  
   **Simpler English:** Her notes about people's practices describe conversations as well as routines.
3. The film presents an ethnographic study of archival work.  
   **Simpler English:** The film studies archival workers' practices through close observation.

**Word family:** ethnography (noun)：民族誌；ethnographically (adverb)：以民族誌方式。  
**Compare two related words:** Observational emphasizes watching; anthropological concerns the broader study of humans. Ethnographic research usually also interprets practices in context.

### 2. Taxonomy — CEFR unverified · noun

**English:** A structured system for classifying things into groups.  
**繁體中文：** 分類系統；分類學。  
**Reference:** [Dictionary reference](https://dictionary.cambridge.org/dictionary/english/taxonomy). No C1/C2 rating verified for the intended meaning.

1. The team developed a taxonomy for historical photographs.  
   **Simpler English:** The team developed an organized system of categories for historical photographs.
2. A useful taxonomy makes related images easier to find.  
   **Simpler English:** A useful category system makes related images easier to find.
3. The taxonomy separates subjects from photographic formats.  
   **Simpler English:** The category system keeps subjects separate from photographic formats.

**Word family:** taxonomic (adjective)：分類學的；taxonomically (adverb)：依分類系統地。  
**Compare two related words:** Classification is the broader act of grouping; a tag is an individual label. A taxonomy organizes categories and their relationships; tags need not follow a hierarchy.

### 3. Arduous — C2 · adjective

**English:** Requiring prolonged, tiring effort.  
**繁體中文：** 艱鉅而費力的。  
**Reference:** [Level and meaning source](https://www.oxfordlearnersdictionaries.com/definition/english/arduous). The source labels the relevant meaning C2.

1. Obtaining permission was an arduous process.  
   **Simpler English:** Getting permission was a long, tiring process.
2. The researchers completed an arduous journey to the facility.  
   **Simpler English:** The researchers completed a difficult, tiring journey to the facility.
3. Cataloguing the damaged boxes proved arduous.  
   **Simpler English:** Listing and describing the damaged boxes took a great deal of tiring effort.

**Word family:** arduously (adverb)：艱辛地；arduousness (noun)：艱鉅程度（較少用）。  
**Compare two related words:** Difficult can concern any challenge; laborious often emphasizes slow, painstaking work. Arduous stresses sustained effort and fatigue.

### 4. Archival — CEFR unverified · adjective

**English:** Relating to records kept for their lasting historical or evidential value.  
**繁體中文：** 檔案的；與保存紀錄有關的。  
**Reference:** [Dictionary reference](https://dictionary.cambridge.org/dictionary/english/archival). No C1/C2 rating verified for the intended meaning.

1. The project combines archival research with photography.  
   **Simpler English:** The project combines research using preserved records with photography.
2. Staff explained the archival procedures to visitors.  
   **Simpler English:** Staff explained the procedures for handling preserved records to visitors.
3. The exhibition includes archival footage.  
   **Simpler English:** The exhibition includes film from preserved records.

**Word family:** archive (noun/verb)：檔案庫／歸檔；archivist (noun)：檔案管理員。  
**Compare two related words:** Historical concerns the past; documentary concerns recorded evidence. Archival describes a relationship to retained records, including recent ones.

### 5. Digitization — CEFR unverified · noun

**English:** Conversion of physical or analogue material into digital data.  
**繁體中文：** 數位化；將實體或類比資料轉為數位資料。  
**Reference:** [Dictionary reference](https://dictionary.cambridge.org/dictionary/english/digitization). No C1/C2 rating verified for the intended meaning.

1. Digitization allowed readers to view the negatives online.  
   **Simpler English:** Converting the negatives into digital files allowed readers to view them online.
2. Careful digitization preserves detail in the original image.  
   **Simpler English:** Careful conversion into digital files keeps details from the original image.
3. Digitization does not automatically produce accurate descriptions.  
   **Simpler English:** Making digital copies does not automatically create accurate descriptions.

**Word family:** digitize/digitise (verb)：數位化；digitized (participial adjective)：已數位化的。  
**Compare two related words:** Scanning is one capture method; digitalization often means changing workflows through digital technology. Digitization focuses on conversion, though usage varies.

**Research phrase:** An ethnographic approach — 民族誌研究取向：observe people's practices in context, usually alongside interpretation and interaction; observing objects alone does not automatically make research ethnographic.

## Two useful phrases — 2 minutes

### Business: Image taxonomy

**English:** A structured classification system for images.  
**繁體中文：** 影像分類系統。  
**Example:** We should agree on the image taxonomy before importing the files.  
**Simpler English:** We should agree on the system for grouping images before importing the files.

### Everyday: At your leisure

**English:** Whenever you have free time, without a rush.  
**繁體中文：** 在你方便、有空時；不必急。  
**Example:** You can watch the film at your leisure.  
**Simpler English:** You can watch the film whenever you have free time; there is no rush.

## Reading — 3 minutes

### Researching an image archive

*An original learning passage based on the supplied notes; explanatory observations are not additional biographical claims.*

The meeting notes describe Lucas Briffa's photography thesis as a study of an archive inside a limestone mine in Boyers, Pennsylvania. Its unusual location immediately attracts attention, but the more interesting question is how images become available to people who were never present when they were made. A photograph can survive physically and still remain difficult to discover.

An **ethnographic** approach shifts attention toward the people and routines that connect storage with use. Instead of looking only at photographs, a researcher can observe how staff handle requests, describe objects, and decide which information belongs in a record. Such observations help explain why an archive is also a workplace.

The notes identify image **taxonomy** as a central concern. Categories are useful because a large collection cannot be searched one object at a time. Yet categories also shape what users encounter. A label may make one subject easy to find while leaving another aspect of the same photograph almost invisible.

Gaining access was described as **arduous**. That difficulty belongs to the story of the research, rather than being evidence that every archive works in the same way. Careful **archival** research requires attention to permissions, institutional context, and the limits of what a visitor can see.

Finally, **digitization** changes the form in which an image circulates. It does not automatically explain the image's history. A digital file still needs a description and a place within an **image taxonomy**.

For a follow-up viewing, a colleague might share the installation films and say, “Watch them **at your leisure**.” Slower viewing can encourage attention to processes that a quick presentation may leave in the background.

**Check your understanding:** Why can a preserved photograph still be hard to find? What does digitization leave unexplained?

**繁體中文提示：** 思考分類、描述與研究情境，而不只看影像是否已被掃描。
