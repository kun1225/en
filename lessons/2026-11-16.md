## Vocabulary — 7 minutes

Read the original examples and their simpler equivalents. Comparisons describe tendencies, not absolute rules. CEFR labels apply to the specified meanings, not automatically to every word-family member. Definitions and examples are original.

### 1. Preliminary — C1 · adjective, usually before a noun

**English:** Done at an early stage to prepare for a fuller activity or final decision.  
**繁體中文：** 初步的；準備性的。  
**Verification:** [Cambridge Dictionary](https://dictionary.cambridge.org/dictionary/english/preliminary) labels this before-the-main-stage meaning **C1**.

1. We held a preliminary meeting to identify the main questions.  
   **Simpler English:** We held an early meeting to work out the main questions before the next stage.
2. The preliminary results suggest that users prefer the simpler layout.  
   **Simpler English:** The early results suggest that users prefer the simpler layout.
3. She prepared a preliminary budget before requesting detailed quotes.  
   **Simpler English:** She prepared an initial budget before asking for detailed prices.

**Word family:** preliminarily *(adverb)*：初步地（較正式，日常常用 initially）；preliminaries *(plural noun)*：準備工作、初步程序。  
**Compare:** **Initial**（最初的）emphasizes being at the beginning; preliminary often also points toward a later, fuller stage. **Provisional**（暫定的）emphasizes being subject to change or confirmation. A preliminary estimate may also be provisional, but the words emphasize different things.  
**Pattern:** preliminary findings / discussions / assessment.

### 2. Comprehensive — C1 · adjective

**English:** Covering the important parts of a subject widely and thoroughly.  
**繁體中文：** 全面的；詳盡周全的。  
**Verification:** [Cambridge Dictionary](https://dictionary.cambridge.org/us/dictionary/english/comprehensive?topic=complete-and-whole) labels this broad-coverage meaning **C1**.

1. We need a comprehensive review of the service before renewing the contract.  
   **Simpler English:** We need to review all the important parts of the service before renewing the contract.
2. The guide offers comprehensive information about local transport.  
   **Simpler English:** The guide gives detailed information covering all the main parts of local transport.
3. Her report was comprehensive without being difficult to follow.  
   **Simpler English:** Her report covered the subject fully and was still easy to follow.

**Derived forms:** comprehensively *(adverb)*：全面地；comprehensiveness *(noun)*：全面性、周全性。  
**Compare:** **Detailed**（詳細的）emphasizes small points; a detailed report can cover only one narrow topic. **Exhaustive**（鉅細靡遺的）makes a stronger claim of leaving nothing out. Comprehensive usually means broad, thorough coverage without guaranteeing literally every detail.  
**Pattern:** a comprehensive review / guide / account.

### 3. Unbiased — C2 · adjective

**English:** Not unfairly influenced by a preference for one person, side, or outcome.  
**繁體中文：** 不偏頗的；公正的。  
**Verification:** [Cambridge Dictionary](https://dictionary.cambridge.org/dictionary/english/unbiased) labels this fair-judgment meaning **C2**.

1. We asked an independent reviewer for an unbiased assessment.  
   **Simpler English:** We asked an independent reviewer to judge fairly without favoring either side.
2. The article aims to give an unbiased account of the dispute.  
   **Simpler English:** The article aims to describe the disagreement fairly without favoring either side.
3. An unbiased comparison should include the weaknesses of both products.  
   **Simpler English:** A fair comparison should include what is wrong with both products, without favoring one.

**Word family:** biased *(adjective; opposite)*：有偏見的；bias *(noun/verb; base)*：偏見／使偏頗。Unbiasedness *(noun)* exists, but “fairness” or “lack of bias” is usually more natural.  
**Compare:** **Impartial**（不偏袒的）often stresses treating competing sides fairly. **Objective**（客觀的）often stresses using facts rather than personal feelings. The meanings overlap; using numbers alone does not guarantee an unbiased comparison if the numbers were selectively chosen.  
**Pattern:** an unbiased assessment / opinion / comparison.

### 4. Conclusive — C2 · adjective

**English:** Strong enough to settle a question by establishing the answer clearly.  
**繁體中文：** 決定性的；足以定論的。  
**Verification:** [Cambridge Dictionary](https://dictionary.cambridge.org/dictionary/english/conclusive?topic=certainty) labels this question-settling meaning **C2**.

1. The experiment provided conclusive evidence that the seal was leaking.  
   **Simpler English:** The experiment gave clear proof that the seal was leaking.
2. The first test was not conclusive, so we repeated it.  
   **Simpler English:** The first test did not settle the question, so we repeated it.
3. We need a conclusive answer before blaming the supplier.  
   **Simpler English:** We need an answer that settles the question before blaming the supplier.

**Word family:** conclusively *(adverb)*：確鑿地、足以定論地；inconclusive *(adjective)*：無法定論的；conclusion *(noun; related base-family form)*：結論。  
**Compare:** **Suggestive**（有暗示性的）points toward an explanation without settling it. **Decisive**（決定性的）often emphasizes determining an outcome or choice; conclusive emphasizes settling uncertainty through evidence. Evidence can influence a decision without being conclusive.  
**Pattern:** conclusive evidence / proof / results.

### 5. Comparative — C1 · adjective

**English:** Examining two or more things to identify their similarities and differences.  
**繁體中文：** 比較性的；用於比較的。  
**Verification:** [Cambridge Dictionary](https://dictionary.cambridge.org/dictionary/english/comparative?topic=comparing-and-contrasting) labels this comparing-things meaning **C1**. This lesson does not teach the grammatical noun “comparative.”

1. We conducted a comparative study of three delivery services.  
   **Simpler English:** We studied three delivery services to see how they were similar and different.
2. Her comparative analysis considered both price and reliability.  
   **Simpler English:** Her analysis compared the options by looking at both price and reliability.
3. The report includes comparative data from five cities.  
   **Simpler English:** The report includes data that lets us compare five cities.

**Word family:** compare *(verb; base)*：比較；comparison *(noun)*：比較；comparatively *(adverb)*：相較而言、相對地（常用於另一個「相對」語義，並非每個 comparative 都能直接改成此副詞）。  
**Compare:** **Comparable**（可比較的、相若的）describes things similar enough for a useful comparison. **Contrasting**（對照的、呈現差異的）often highlights differences. A comparative study can examine similarities as well as differences between comparable options.  
**Pattern:** a comparative study / analysis / review.

## Two useful phrases — 2 minutes

### Business: Weigh up the options

**English:** Consider the advantages and disadvantages of the available choices before deciding.  
**繁體中文：** 權衡各個選項。  
**Example:** Let's weigh up the options before committing to a two-year contract.  
**Simpler English:** Let's consider the good and bad points of each choice before agreeing to a two-year contract.  
**Usage:** “Weigh up” is especially common in British English; “weigh the options” is also natural.

### Everyday life: Keep an open mind

**English:** Remain willing to consider different ideas rather than decide too early.  
**繁體中文：** 保持開放態度；先不要預設立場。  
**Example:** I prefer the smaller apartment, but I'll keep an open mind until we've seen both.  
**Simpler English:** I prefer the smaller apartment, but I'm willing to consider either one until we've seen both.  
**Usage:** Being open to an option does not mean you must eventually choose it.

## Reading — 3 minutes

### A fair test before a big purchase

*Original fictional article.*

A neighborhood library wanted to replace its old online catalogue. Two suppliers offered similar prices, but their demonstrations emphasized different features. One showed an attractive search page; the other focused on tools for staff. Rather than choose the more impressive presentation, the library manager, Priya, asked her team to **weigh up the options**.

They began with a **preliminary** checklist: readers needed to find books easily, staff needed to update records quickly, and both groups needed reliable access. Priya then arranged a **comparative** trial using the same tasks on both systems. This made the results easier to interpret than two unrelated demonstrations.

To keep the review **unbiased**, she invited staff who preferred different suppliers and asked them to record problems as well as successes. They also included several readers who had not seen either presentation. Everyone completed the tasks before discussing their opinions.

The first results favored the system with the simpler search page. However, Priya warned that a small trial was not **conclusive**. One reader had used a slow internet connection, and the team had not yet tested large batches of records.

A more **comprehensive** review followed. Staff checked data transfers, support arrangements, training, and the costs of leaving the service later. Several weaknesses appeared that the sales demonstrations had not covered.

Priya personally preferred one supplier, but she encouraged everyone to **keep an open mind**. By the end of the week, the team had chosen a system for clear reasons and identified questions to resolve before signing.

The process took longer than a quick vote. It also gave the library something more valuable than an attractive promise: a shared understanding of what the new system could and could not do.

**理解檢查：** Why did the team use the same tasks for both systems? Why weren't the first results conclusive?  
**繁體中文提示：** 留意比較條件是否一致，以及小規模測試尚未涵蓋的情況。
