## Vocabulary — 7 minutes

Read the examples and use the simpler versions to check meaning. Comparisons describe tendencies, not strict boundaries. CEFR levels apply to the specified meanings, not automatically to derived forms. Definitions and examples are original.

### 1. Constructive — C1 · adjective

**English:** Intended to help improve something, rather than merely point out faults.  
**繁體中文：** 有建設性的；有助於改善的。  
**Verification:** [Cambridge Dictionary](https://dictionary.cambridge.org/dictionary/english/constructive) labels the helpful-advice-or-criticism meaning **C1**.

1. Her constructive feedback helped me improve the opening paragraph.  
   **Simpler English:** Her helpful comments showed me how to make the opening paragraph better.
2. We need a constructive discussion about what went wrong.  
   **Simpler English:** We need to discuss what went wrong in a way that helps us improve.
3. He offered a constructive suggestion instead of simply rejecting the plan.  
   **Simpler English:** He suggested a way to improve the plan instead of just saying no.

**Derived forms:** constructively *(adverb)*：有建設性地；unconstructive *(adjective)*：無助於改善的。  
**Compare:** **Helpful**（有幫助的）is broader and can describe any useful assistance. **Positive**（正面的）often suggests approval or optimism. Constructive feedback can contain criticism: its purpose is improvement, so it need not be entirely positive.  
**Pattern:** constructive criticism / feedback / discussion.

### 2. Tactful — C2 · adjective

**English:** Careful in how you speak or act so that you avoid unnecessarily upsetting people.  
**繁體中文：** 得體的；說話做事顧及他人感受的。  
**Verification:** [Cambridge Dictionary](https://dictionary.cambridge.org/dictionary/english/tactful) labels this considerate-communication meaning **C2**.

1. She found a tactful way to mention the spelling mistakes.  
   **Simpler English:** She mentioned the spelling mistakes in a way that avoided hurting anyone's feelings.
2. His tactful reply prevented the conversation from becoming an argument.  
   **Simpler English:** His careful, considerate reply stopped the conversation from turning into an argument.
3. A tactful manager can discuss poor performance without humiliating an employee.  
   **Simpler English:** A manager who chooses words carefully can discuss poor work without making the employee feel ashamed.

**Word family:** tact *(noun; base)*：分寸、應對技巧；tactfully *(adverb)*：得體地；tactless *(adjective)*：不得體的、不顧感受的。  
**Compare:** **Polite**（有禮貌的）often concerns manners; tactful emphasizes sensitivity in a delicate situation. **Diplomatic**（圓融的）often emphasizes managing disagreement or competing interests. Both can describe a skillful response; tactful especially highlights avoiding hurt feelings.  
**Pattern:** a tactful response; be tactful about something.

### 3. Receptive — C2 · adjective

**English:** Willing to listen to and consider new ideas or suggestions.  
**繁體中文：** 願意聆聽並接受新想法的；有接受意願的。  
**Verification:** [Cambridge Dictionary](https://dictionary.cambridge.org/dictionary/english/receptive) labels this openness-to-suggestions meaning **C2**.

1. The team was receptive to suggestions from new members.  
   **Simpler English:** The team was willing to listen to and consider new members' suggestions.
2. She seemed more receptive after I explained the purpose of the change.  
   **Simpler English:** She seemed more willing to consider the change after I explained its purpose.
3. A receptive audience can still ask difficult questions.  
   **Simpler English:** People who are willing to consider your ideas can still ask difficult questions.

**Derived forms:** receptiveness *(noun)*：接受意願、開放態度；receptively *(adverb)*：以願意接受的態度（較少用）。  
**Compare:** **Open-minded**（思想開放的）often describes a general willingness to consider different views; receptive can describe someone's response to one particular proposal. **Agreeable**（願意同意的、可接受的）can suggest actual agreement. Being receptive does not mean you have already agreed.  
**Pattern:** receptive to + noun; not “receptive for.”

### 4. Defensive — C2 · adjective

**English:** Too quick to justify or protect yourself because you feel criticized.  
**繁體中文：** 因感到受批評而急於辯解的；有防衛心的。  
**Verification:** [Cambridge Dictionary, SENSITIVE meaning](https://dictionary.cambridge.org/dictionary/english/defensive?topic=denying-and-contradicting) labels this response-to-criticism meaning **C2**.

1. He became defensive when I asked about the missed deadline.  
   **Simpler English:** He felt criticized and started defending himself when I asked about the missed deadline.
2. Her defensive response made it harder to discuss the mistake.  
   **Simpler English:** The way she quickly defended herself made the mistake harder to discuss.
3. I try not to get defensive when someone questions my work.  
   **Simpler English:** I try not to react as if I am being attacked when someone questions my work.

**Derived forms:** defensively *(adverb)*：帶著防衛心地；defensiveness *(noun)*：防衛心理、急於自我辯護的態度。  
**Compare:** **Protective**（保護的）often focuses on keeping someone or something safe, without implying oversensitivity. **Hostile**（有敵意的）suggests antagonism. A defensive response may be an anxious explanation rather than an openly hostile attack.  
**Pattern:** get / become defensive; a defensive response.

### 5. Fearful — C2 · adjective · formal

**English:** Feeling afraid or worried that something bad may happen.  
**繁體中文：** 害怕的；擔憂的。  
**Verification:** [Cambridge Dictionary](https://dictionary.cambridge.org/dictionary/english/fearful) marks this afraid-or-worried meaning **C2** and labels it formal.

1. Junior staff were fearful of making mistakes in front of the director.  
   **Simpler English:** Junior staff were afraid of making mistakes in front of the director.
2. She was fearful that honest feedback might damage their friendship.  
   **Simpler English:** She was worried that honest feedback might harm their friendship.
3. He sounded fearful about the changes to his role.  
   **Simpler English:** He sounded worried about the changes to his job.

**Word family:** fear *(noun/verb; base)*：恐懼／害怕；fearfully *(adverb)*：害怕地；fearfulness *(noun)*：恐懼、膽怯。  
**Compare:** **Afraid**（害怕的）is the more everyday choice. **Anxious**（焦慮的）often stresses uneasy anticipation rather than fear itself. Fearful is relatively formal and commonly points toward a feared outcome; all three can overlap.  
**Pattern:** fearful of + noun/-ing; fearful that + clause.

## Two useful phrases — 2 minutes

### Business: Touch base

**English:** Make brief contact to exchange updates or check progress.  
**繁體中文：** 簡短聯繫；碰一下進度。  
**Example:** Let's touch base on Thursday to see whether the revised draft is ready.  
**Simpler English:** Let's have a quick conversation on Thursday to check whether the revised draft is ready.  
**Usage:** Conversational workplace English; touch base with someone about something.

### Everyday life: Clear the air

**English:** Talk openly about a disagreement or misunderstanding to reduce tension.  
**繁體中文：** 把話說開；消除誤會或隔閡。  
**Example:** We talked after dinner to clear the air about yesterday's argument.  
**Simpler English:** We talked after dinner to deal with the bad feelings from yesterday's argument.  
**Usage:** Useful between friends, family members, or colleagues; it suggests addressing tension, not simply chatting.

## Reading — 3 minutes

### A conversation worth having

*Original fictional article.*

Lena and her friend Daniel were preparing a website for a local photography exhibition. Daniel had written the introduction, while Lena organized the images. When she read his draft, she noticed that visitors might struggle to understand where the exhibition would take place. She wanted to help, but she was **fearful** that criticism might upset him.

Her first message was too brief: “The introduction needs work.” Daniel became **defensive** and replied that he had spent his entire evening writing it. Lena realized that her comment sounded like a judgment without offering a way forward.

She asked whether they could meet to **clear the air**. Over coffee, she explained that she appreciated his effort and wanted the page to answer visitors' questions quickly. She then gave **constructive** feedback: move the location and opening hours into the first paragraph, and save the exhibition's history for a later section.

Daniel was more **receptive** once he understood the purpose of the changes. He explained that the exhibition organizer had specifically requested the historical background. Together, they found a solution that included both practical details and the story behind the photographs.

Lena also chose a more **tactful** way to discuss a confusing sentence. Instead of calling it badly written, she asked what Daniel wanted readers to understand. His explanation helped them find clearer wording without turning the discussion into a contest.

Before leaving, they agreed to **touch base** the following afternoon. Daniel would revise the introduction, and Lena would check whether the page was easy to read on a phone.

The conversation did more than improve the website. It showed them that useful feedback requires both clear suggestions and a willingness to understand the other person's priorities.

**理解檢查：** Why did Daniel initially become defensive? What made Lena's later feedback constructive?  
**繁體中文提示：** 比較籠統批評與具體修改建議，也留意聆聽對方需求的作用。
