## Vocabulary — 7 minutes

Read each example and its simpler version. Comparisons describe common tendencies, not strict boundaries. CEFR ratings apply to the stated meanings, not automatically to word-family members. Definitions and examples are original.

### 1. Allocate — C1 · verb, transitive

**English:** Decide how much time, money, space, or another resource to give to a particular purpose.  
**繁體中文：** 分配；撥出。  
**Verification:** [Cambridge Dictionary](https://dictionary.cambridge.org/us/dictionary/english/allocate) marks this resource-distribution meaning **C1**.

1. We allocated two hours to reviewing customer feedback.  
   **Simpler English:** We set aside two hours to review customer feedback.
2. The school allocated more money to language courses.  
   **Simpler English:** The school set aside more money for language courses.
3. Each group was allocated a separate room.  
   **Simpler English:** Each group was given its own room.

**Word family:** allocation *(noun)*：分配、配額；reallocate *(verb)*：重新分配。  
**Compare:** **Assign**（指派）often links a task or role to a person; allocate often divides resources. **Distribute**（分發、分配）emphasizes spreading things among recipients. Allocation can be a planning decision before anything is physically distributed.  
**Pattern:** allocate something to someone/something; allocate time for an activity.

### 2. Delegate — C2 · verb, intransitive or transitive

**English:** Give someone else part of your work or responsibility to carry out.  
**繁體中文：** 委派；授權他人處理。  
**Verification:** [Cambridge Dictionary, GIVE meaning](https://dictionary.cambridge.org/us/dictionary/english/delegate) labels this transfer-of-work meaning **C2**.

1. She delegated the travel arrangements to her assistant.  
   **Simpler English:** She gave her assistant responsibility for arranging the travel.
2. New managers sometimes find it difficult to delegate.  
   **Simpler English:** New managers sometimes find it hard to let others take responsibility for some of their work.
3. I delegated the weekly report so I could focus on hiring.  
   **Simpler English:** I gave someone else responsibility for the weekly report so I could focus on hiring.

**Word family:** delegation *(noun)*：委派、授權；delegated *(participial adjective)*：獲授予的，如 delegated authority（獲授予的權限）。  
**Compare:** **Allocate**（分配）can concern money, time, or tasks; delegate specifically concerns handing work or authority to someone. **Abdicate**（放棄職責）often criticizes failing to accept responsibility. Delegating work usually does not remove your responsibility for overseeing the result.  
**Pattern:** delegate a task to someone; learn to delegate.

### 3. Overwhelm — C1 · verb, transitive, often passive

**English:** Give someone more work, information, or difficulty than they can comfortably handle.  
**繁體中文：** 使不堪負荷；使應接不暇。  
**Verification:** [Cambridge Dictionary, BE TOO MUCH meaning](https://dictionary.cambridge.org/us/dictionary/english/overwhelm) marks this too-much-to-manage meaning **C1**.

1. The number of requests overwhelmed our small team.  
   **Simpler English:** There were more requests than our small team could handle.
2. Don't overwhelm new employees with information on their first day.  
   **Simpler English:** Don't give new employees more information than they can handle on their first day.
3. I felt overwhelmed by the amount of work waiting for me.  
   **Simpler English:** I felt there was more work waiting for me than I could manage.

**Word family:** overwhelmed *(participial adjective)*：不堪負荷的；overwhelming *(adjective)*：令人難以招架的、壓倒性的；overwhelmingly *(adverb)*：壓倒性地（常用於多數或強度，不一定表示工作負荷）。  
**Compare:** **Overload**（使超載）often emphasizes exceeding capacity; overwhelm often highlights the resulting inability to cope. **Stress**（使有壓力）concerns strain and worry, even when someone can still manage. Their uses overlap.  
**Pattern:** feel overwhelmed by/with something; overwhelm someone with information.

### 4. Versatile — C2 · adjective

**English:** Able to do many different things well, or serve several different purposes.  
**繁體中文：** 多才多藝的；多用途的。  
**Verification:** [Cambridge Dictionary](https://dictionary.cambridge.org/dictionary/english/versatile) labels this range-of-abilities-or-uses meaning **C2**.

1. She's a versatile designer who works on websites and packaging.  
   **Simpler English:** She's a designer who can do different kinds of work well, including websites and packaging.
2. We need a versatile room for meetings, classes, and exhibitions.  
   **Simpler English:** We need a room that works well for meetings, classes, and exhibitions.
3. This versatile tool can cut, sand, and polish.  
   **Simpler English:** This tool has several uses: it can cut, sand, and polish.

**Derived form:** versatility *(noun)*：多才多藝、多功能性。  
**Compare:** **Flexible**（靈活的）often emphasizes adjusting arrangements or methods; versatile emphasizes a range of abilities or uses. **Specialized**（專門的）emphasizes a particular purpose or field. Someone may be versatile across several tasks while also having specialized expertise.  
**Pattern:** a versatile performer / tool / space.

### 5. Competent — C1 · adjective

**English:** Having enough skill and knowledge to do something properly.  
**繁體中文：** 能勝任的；具備足夠能力的。  
**Verification:** [Cambridge Dictionary](https://dictionary.cambridge.org/dictionary/english/competent) marks this sufficient-skill meaning **C1**.

1. We need a competent editor to check the final version.  
   **Simpler English:** We need an editor with enough skill to check the final version properly.
2. After training, she was competent to handle routine enquiries.  
   **Simpler English:** After training, she had the skills needed to deal with ordinary questions.
3. He's competent at preparing budgets but still learning to negotiate.  
   **Simpler English:** He can prepare budgets properly, but he's still learning to negotiate.

**Word family:** competence *(noun)*：勝任能力；competently *(adverb)*：稱職地；incompetent *(adjective)*：不稱職的。  
**Compare:** **Capable**（有能力的）often emphasizes ability or potential; competent suggests meeting the required standard. **Expert**（專業精通的）usually implies a much higher level of knowledge or skill. Competent is positive but can sound like modest praise rather than excellence.  
**Pattern:** competent at/in something; competent to do something.

## Two useful phrases — 2 minutes

### Business: Get the ball rolling

**English:** Take the first action that starts an activity or process.  
**繁體中文：** 開個頭；開始推動事情。  
**Example:** I'll get the ball rolling by sending the team a draft schedule.  
**Simpler English:** I'll start the process by sending the team a draft schedule.  
**Usage:** Conversational workplace English; name the first action when possible.

### Everyday life: Bite off more than you can chew

**English:** Accept more work or responsibility than you can manage.  
**繁體中文：** 承擔超出能力負荷的事；攬太多事情。  
**Example:** I think I've bitten off more than I can chew by planning two parties this weekend.  
**Simpler English:** I think I've taken on more than I can manage by planning two parties this weekend.  
**Usage:** Informal; often used when admitting you need to reduce a commitment.

## Reading — 3 minutes

### Everyone needs a part

*Original fictional article.*

When Sam agreed to organize a weekend book exchange, he imagined a few tables and a box of donated novels. Within days, however, neighbors offered hundreds of books, a school requested a children's corner, and a café proposed serving drinks. The growing list of requests began to **overwhelm** him.

At a planning meeting, Sam admitted, “I may have **bitten off more than I can chew**.” His friend Iris suggested starting with the work rather than the worries. They listed every task, from collecting donations to returning borrowed chairs, and estimated how long each would take.

First, they needed to **allocate** their limited resources. They reserved the largest room for browsing and set aside part of the budget for signs. Then Sam learned to **delegate**. He gave Iris responsibility for volunteer schedules and asked another neighbor to manage book deliveries. Each person knew what decisions they could make and when to ask for help.

Their most **versatile** volunteer, Jo, could design posters, repair shelves, and welcome visitors. Instead of asking Jo to do everything, they chose the tasks where those skills mattered most. A **competent** student volunteer took charge of registration after a short practice session.

To **get the ball rolling**, Sam sent everyone a simple checklist that evening. It included a named owner, a deadline, and one clear result for each task. The list made unanswered questions visible before they became urgent problems.

On Saturday, Sam still had plenty to do, but he no longer had to answer every question himself. The event succeeded because people understood their roles, not because one organizer worked without stopping. Sharing responsibility had made the project easier to manage and more enjoyable for everyone.

**理解檢查：** Why didn't the team give every difficult task to Jo? How did the checklist support delegation?  
**繁體中文提示：** 多才多藝不代表應承擔所有工作；清楚列出負責人、期限與成果，有助於分工。
