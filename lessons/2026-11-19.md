## Vocabulary — 7 minutes

Read each example and its simpler version. Comparisons show common tendencies, not absolute boundaries. CEFR labels apply to the specified meanings, not automatically to derived forms. Definitions and examples are original.

### 1. Setback — C1 · countable noun

**English:** A problem or event that delays progress or makes success harder.  
**繁體中文：** 挫折；使進展受阻的事件。  
**Verification:** [Cambridge Dictionary](https://dictionary.cambridge.org/us/dictionary/english/setback?topic=difficult-things-and-people) marks this progress-delaying meaning **C1**.

1. Losing our main supplier was a serious setback for the project.  
   **Simpler English:** Losing our main supplier seriously delayed the project and made success harder.
2. The team suffered a setback when the first test failed.  
   **Simpler English:** The failed first test slowed the team's progress.
3. One setback doesn't mean the whole plan has failed.  
   **Simpler English:** One problem that slows progress doesn't mean the whole plan has failed.

**Related form:** set someone/something back *(phrasal verb)*：使某人或某事的進展延誤。Setbacks is the plural, not a derivative; there is no common everyday adjective derived directly from setback.  
**Compare:** **Failure**（失敗）can mean an unsuccessful outcome; a setback may delay success without ending the effort. **Obstacle**（障礙）is something in the way; a setback emphasizes the loss of progress caused by an event. An obstacle can cause a setback.  
**Pattern:** suffer a setback; a temporary / major setback.

### 2. Disruption — C1 · countable or uncountable noun

**English:** An interruption that prevents an activity or system from continuing normally.  
**繁體中文：** 中斷；對正常運作的干擾。  
**Verification:** [Cambridge Dictionary](https://dictionary.cambridge.org/dictionary/english/disruption) marks this interrupted-operation meaning **C1**.

1. The power cut caused disruption to the evening classes.  
   **Simpler English:** The power cut stopped the evening classes from running normally.
2. We moved the meeting online to reduce disruption.  
   **Simpler English:** We moved the meeting online so it would interfere less with normal activities.
3. Staff warned customers about possible disruptions to deliveries.  
   **Simpler English:** Staff warned customers that deliveries might not run normally.

**Word family:** disrupt *(verb; base)*：擾亂、中斷；disruptive *(adjective)*：造成干擾的；disruptively *(adverb)*：以干擾他人的方式。  
**Compare:** **Interruption**（中斷）can be a brief pause; disruption often suggests a disturbance to normal functioning. **Delay**（延誤）focuses on something happening later than planned. A disruption may cause delays, cancellations, or changes rather than simply a later start.  
**Pattern:** disruption to services; cause / minimize disruption.

### 3. Adverse — C2 · adjective, usually before a noun

**English:** Having an unfavorable or harmful effect on a situation or outcome.  
**繁體中文：** 不利的；有害的。  
**Verification:** [Cambridge Dictionary](https://dictionary.cambridge.org/dictionary/english/adverse?topic=dangerous-and-harmful) labels this negative-effect meaning **C2**.

1. Adverse weather conditions forced us to change the route.  
   **Simpler English:** Bad weather conditions forced us to change the route.
2. The price increase had an adverse effect on demand.  
   **Simpler English:** The price increase harmed demand.
3. The team continued working under adverse conditions.  
   **Simpler English:** The team continued working in difficult, unfavorable conditions.

**Word family:** adversely *(adverb)*：不利地、有害地；adversity *(noun)*：逆境、困境。  
**Compare:** **Unfavorable**（不利的）is close in meaning and can also describe a negative opinion. **Averse**（不願意的、反感的）describes someone's attitude, usually averse to something. Adverse conditions make things harder; being averse to change means disliking or resisting change.  
**Pattern:** adverse effects / conditions; adversely affect something.

### 4. Resilience — C2 · uncountable noun

**English:** The ability to recover and function well again after difficulty or disappointment.  
**繁體中文：** 復原力；從困難中恢復的能力。  
**Verification:** [Cambridge Dictionary](https://dictionary.cambridge.org/dictionary/english/resilience) explicitly labels this recovery-after-difficulty meaning **C2**.

1. The team's resilience helped it recover after losing the contract.  
   **Simpler English:** The team's ability to recover helped it get going again after losing the contract.
2. She showed remarkable resilience after her business closed.  
   **Simpler English:** She recovered unusually well after her business closed.
3. Support from friends can help people build resilience.  
   **Simpler English:** Support from friends can help people become better able to recover from difficulties.

**Word family:** resilient *(adjective)*：有復原力的；resiliently *(adverb)*：展現復原力地（較少用）；resiliency *(noun)*：復原力，resilience 較常見。  
**Compare:** **Persistence**（堅持）emphasizes continuing to try; resilience emphasizes recovering after a blow. **Endurance**（耐力）emphasizes sustaining effort or tolerating difficulty over time. They can work together: someone may endure a difficult period, recover, and continue trying.  
**Pattern:** show / build resilience; resilience in the face of difficulty.

### 5. Refine — C2 · transitive verb

**English:** Improve an idea, method, or design through small, careful changes.  
**繁體中文：** 改良；精進；細部調整。  
**Verification:** [Cambridge Dictionary](https://dictionary.cambridge.org/dictionary/english/refine?topic=making-things-better) labels this small-improvements meaning **C2**, distinct from purifying a substance.

1. We refined the instructions after watching new users struggle.  
   **Simpler English:** We made small improvements to the instructions after seeing new users have trouble.
2. She used the feedback to refine her presentation.  
   **Simpler English:** She used the feedback to improve the details of her presentation.
3. The team will refine the process over the next month.  
   **Simpler English:** The team will make small improvements to the process over the next month.

**Word family:** refinement *(noun)*：細部改良、精進；refined *(participial adjective)*：經改良的，如 a refined method。Refined can also mean cultured or purified in other contexts.  
**Compare:** **Revise**（修改）can involve substantial changes or simple corrections. **Overhaul**（全面整頓）suggests major, thorough changes. Refine usually improves something's details while keeping its basic structure, though the boundaries overlap.  
**Pattern:** refine a method / design / approach.

## Two useful phrases — 2 minutes

### Business: Get back on track

**English:** Return to the intended plan or begin making the expected progress again.  
**繁體中文：** 重回正軌；恢復原定進度。  
**Example:** We need to confirm the new delivery dates to get the project back on track.  
**Simpler English:** We need to confirm the new delivery dates so the project can make progress as planned again.  
**Usage:** get something back on track; a project is back on track.

### Everyday life: Roll with the punches

**English:** Adjust to difficulties or unexpected changes instead of letting them stop you.  
**繁體中文：** 隨機應變；面對挫折時調整步伐。  
**Example:** Our train was cancelled, so we rolled with the punches and took a bus.  
**Simpler English:** Our train was cancelled, so we adjusted to the problem and took a bus.  
**Usage:** Informal; often encourages flexibility, but can sound dismissive if someone needs practical help.

## Reading — 3 minutes

### A different opening day

*Original fictional article.*

Three days before a neighborhood art studio was due to open, a storm damaged the building's entrance. The damage was a major **setback**: the team had invited local families, printed posters, and booked several teachers. Cancelling the whole event seemed possible, but the organizer, Amira, first asked what could still happen safely.

The **adverse** weather had also affected nearby roads. Even if the entrance was repaired, some visitors would struggle to arrive. Amira contacted the building manager and learned that a side room had a separate, undamaged entrance. It was smaller than the main hall but suitable for two workshops.

The team reduced the programme and offered later dates to everyone whose session could not run. To limit **disruption**, they posted one clear update, emailed ticket holders, and placed a notice at the original entrance. Each message explained the changes and how to request a refund.

Amira wanted to **get the project back on track**, but she avoided promising an immediate return to the original schedule. First, the team needed reliable repair dates. They also used the experience to **refine** their opening checklist, adding a contact list and a simple process for announcing changes.

On opening morning, a teacher discovered that some supplies were missing. Rather than panic, she decided to **roll with the punches** and adapted the activity to use paper already available in the room.

The event was smaller than planned, yet visitors enjoyed the workshops. For Amira, the team's **resilience** was visible in how people supported one another, acknowledged disappointment, and found a practical next step. Recovering did not mean pretending nothing had happened. It meant continuing with a plan that matched the new circumstances.

**理解檢查：** Why didn't Amira promise an immediate return to the original schedule? How did the team refine its checklist?  
**繁體中文提示：** 留意可靠的修復日期，以及聯絡名單與公告流程的改善。
