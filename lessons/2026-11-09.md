## Vocabulary — 7 minutes

Read each original sentence, then its simpler version. CEFR levels apply to the intended meanings, not automatically to derived forms. Comparisons describe tendencies; meanings can overlap.

### 1. Plausible — C2 · adjective

**English:** Seeming reasonable enough to believe, without necessarily being proven true.  
**繁體中文：** 貌似合理的；聽起來可信的。  
**Verification:** [Cambridge Dictionary](https://dictionary.cambridge.org/dictionary/english/plausible?q=plausible_1) labels this believable-explanation meaning **C2**.

1. She offered a plausible explanation for the missing invoice.  
   **Simpler English:** She gave an explanation for the missing bill that sounded reasonable.
2. A delivery error is a plausible cause of the shortage.  
   **Simpler English:** A delivery mistake could reasonably explain why there isn't enough stock.
3. His excuse sounded plausible, but we still checked the dates.  
   **Simpler English:** His excuse sounded believable, but we still checked the dates.

**Word family:** plausibility *(noun)*：可信性、合理性；plausibly *(adverb)*：看似合理地；implausible *(adjective)*：難以置信的。  
**Compare:** **Credible**（可信的）often emphasizes deserving belief, for example because a source is reliable; plausible often concerns whether an explanation makes sense. **Probable**（很可能的）makes a stronger claim about likelihood. A plausible explanation is not necessarily the most probable one.  
**Pattern:** a plausible explanation / excuse / account.

### 2. Arbitrary — C2 · adjective

**English:** Chosen without a clear guiding reason or consistent principle.  
**繁體中文：** 任意的；缺乏明確依據的。  
**Verification:** [Cambridge Dictionary](https://dictionary.cambridge.org/dictionary/english/arbitrary?topic=chance-and-randomness) labels this reason-or-principle meaning **C2**.

1. The team questioned the arbitrary deadline.  
   **Simpler English:** The team questioned the deadline because there seemed to be no clear reason for it.
2. The fee seems arbitrary because similar customers pay different amounts.  
   **Simpler English:** The fee seems to have no clear basis because similar customers pay different amounts.
3. We should avoid arbitrary changes to the scoring system.  
   **Simpler English:** We should avoid changing the scoring system without a clear reason or rule.

**Derived forms:** arbitrarily *(adverb)*：任意地；arbitrariness *(noun)*：任意性、缺乏一致依據的性質。  
**Compare:** **Random**（隨機的）emphasizes chance, sometimes through a carefully designed process. **Subjective**（主觀的）emphasizes personal judgment. An arbitrary choice may reflect someone's preference rather than chance; a subjective judgment can still have clear reasons.  
**Pattern:** an arbitrary decision / limit / deadline.

### 3. Skeptical — C2 · adjective

**English:** Doubting whether a claim is true or whether something will work.  
**繁體中文：** 持懷疑態度的；不輕易相信的。  
**Verification:** [Cambridge Dictionary](https://dictionary.cambridge.org/dictionary/english/skeptical?topic=suspecting-and-questioning) marks this doubt meaning **C2**. British spelling: **sceptical**.

1. I'm skeptical about the promise of instant results.  
   **Simpler English:** I doubt the claim that we'll get results immediately.
2. The manager remained skeptical after the demonstration.  
   **Simpler English:** The manager still had doubts after seeing how it worked.
3. Customers are skeptical of reviews that sound like advertisements.  
   **Simpler English:** Customers doubt reviews that sound like advertisements.

**Word family:** skepticism / scepticism *(noun)*：懷疑態度；skeptically / sceptically *(adverb)*：懷疑地。  
**Compare:** **Doubtful**（懷疑的、不確定的）is a broader everyday choice. **Cynical**（認為他人自私或動機不純的）often questions people's motives. Skeptical commonly questions a claim's truth; it need not imply distrust of the speaker's intentions.  
**Pattern:** skeptical about / of something.

### 4. Persistent — C2 · adjective

**English:** Continuing to try or act despite difficulty or opposition.  
**繁體中文：** 堅持不懈的；不輕易放棄的。  
**Verification:** [Cambridge Dictionary](https://dictionary.cambridge.org/us/dictionary/english/persistent) labels the determined-person meaning **C2**. This lesson uses that meaning, rather than the separate “lasting a long time” meaning.

1. Her persistent efforts finally secured a meeting with the director.  
   **Simpler English:** She kept trying and finally got a meeting with the director.
2. He was persistent in asking for a written explanation.  
   **Simpler English:** He kept asking for a written explanation.
3. A persistent researcher may try several methods before finding an answer.  
   **Simpler English:** A researcher who keeps trying may test several methods before finding an answer.

**Word family:** persist *(verb; base)*：堅持；persistence *(noun)*：毅力、堅持；persistently *(adverb)*：持續不懈地。  
**Compare:** **Determined**（下定決心的）emphasizes strong intention; persistent emphasizes repeated or continued action. **Stubborn**（固執的）often criticizes refusing to change. Persistence can be praised or criticized depending on whether continuing is appropriate.  
**Pattern:** persistent efforts; persistent in doing something.

### 5. Strenuous — C2 · adjective

**English:** Requiring or using a great deal of physical or mental energy.  
**繁體中文：** 費力的；耗費大量體力或心力的。  
**Verification:** [Cambridge Dictionary](https://dictionary.cambridge.org/dictionary/english/strenuous) labels this effort-and-energy meaning **C2**.

1. Carrying the equipment upstairs was strenuous work.  
   **Simpler English:** Carrying the equipment upstairs took a lot of physical effort.
2. She made strenuous efforts to persuade the committee.  
   **Simpler English:** She tried very hard to persuade the committee.
3. The route includes two strenuous climbs.  
   **Simpler English:** The route includes two climbs that take a lot of energy.

**Derived form:** strenuously *(adverb)*：奮力地、極力地。The noun strenuousness exists but is uncommon; “a lot of effort” is usually more natural.  
**Compare:** **Demanding**（要求高的、吃力的）can concern skill, time, or attention as well as energy. **Exhausting**（令人筋疲力盡的）emphasizes the resulting tiredness. Strenuous emphasizes the effort involved; an activity may be strenuous without leaving everyone exhausted.  
**Pattern:** strenuous exercise / work / efforts.

## Two useful phrases — 2 minutes

### Business: Put something on hold

**English:** Temporarily pause an activity or delay a decision.  
**繁體中文：** 暫緩；暫時擱置。  
**Example:** Let's put the purchase on hold until we have compared the maintenance costs.  
**Simpler English:** Let's delay the purchase until we have compared the maintenance costs.  
**Usage:** put + activity/decision + on hold. It usually suggests a pause, rather than permanent cancellation.

### Everyday life: Take a rain check

**English:** Decline an invitation now while expressing interest in accepting it another time.  
**繁體中文：** 這次先不了，下次再約。  
**Example:** Can I take a rain check on dinner? I'd like to go another evening.  
**Simpler English:** Can we have dinner another evening instead?  
**Usage:** Informal and friendly; it expresses interest in a later occasion without fixing a new date.

## Reading — 3 minutes

### The questions that changed the plan

*Original fictional article.*

A small outdoor club wanted to buy a booking system for its guided walks. The supplier promised that the software would reduce administration by half. Maya, who organized the walks, thought the claim sounded **plausible**. Volunteers spent hours answering the same questions, so automatic reminders might genuinely help.

Still, she was **skeptical** about the promised savings. The demonstration showed only a simple booking, while the club often changed routes because of weather. She asked how the system handled refunds and late cancellations. The salesperson answered some questions but suggested discussing the others after payment.

Maya was **persistent**. She requested written answers and asked another walking club about its experience. That club liked the software but had discovered extra charges for sending messages. These costs did not make the product useless, but they changed the comparison with cheaper options.

The supplier also demanded a decision by Friday. The deadline seemed **arbitrary** because installation could not begin for another month. Maya asked what would change after Friday and received no clear explanation. The committee agreed to **put the purchase on hold** until it understood the full costs.

That evening, a friend invited Maya to dinner. She asked to **take a rain check** because she needed an early night before leading a walk. The next day's route included a **strenuous** climb, and she wanted enough energy to support the group.

By Monday, the supplier had provided a complete price list and extended the offer. Maya had not rejected a useful tool simply because she had doubts. She had asked questions, checked the answers, and given the club a better basis for deciding.

**理解檢查：** Why did the deadline seem arbitrary? How did Maya's persistence help the club?  
**繁體中文提示：** 留意期限缺乏明確理由，以及追問後取得的完整費用資訊。
