## Vocabulary — 7 minutes

All five words are adjectives. Comparisons describe tendencies, not fixed boundaries. The first four requested words and quaint have not appeared as taught vocabulary in the checked lessons. Definitions and examples are original.

### 1. Understated — CEFR unverified

**English:** Restrained and not showy, often in a way that feels elegant or effective.  
**繁體中文：** 低調的；含蓄而不張揚的。  
**Source:** [Cambridge Dictionary](https://dictionary.cambridge.org/dictionary/english/understated). Often approving.

1. The hotel's understated design uses warm wood and soft lighting.  
   **Simpler English:** The hotel's design is simple and not showy, with warm wood and soft lighting.
2. Her understated performance made the final scene especially moving.  
   **Simpler English:** Her quiet, restrained acting made the final scene especially moving.
3. He wore an understated jacket without any visible logos.  
   **Simpler English:** He wore a simple, not-showy jacket without any visible logos.

**Word family:** understate *(verb)*：輕描淡寫、淡化；understatement *(noun)*：輕描淡寫的說法或表達。These often mean making something seem less significant than it is; an understated design need not misrepresent anything.  
**Compare:** **Minimalist**（極簡的）emphasizes reducing elements; understated emphasizes restraint and lack of display. **Unpretentious**（不做作的）emphasizes not claiming special importance. A richly furnished room can still feel understated if it avoids showiness.

### 2. Whimsical — CEFR unverified

**English:** Unusually playful or imaginative, sometimes in an amusingly odd way.  
**繁體中文：** 異想天開的；帶有俏皮奇趣的。  
**Source:** [Cambridge Dictionary](https://dictionary.cambridge.org/dictionary/english/whimsical). Often positive in design descriptions, but oddness may also seem annoying.

1. The illustrator drew a whimsical city where cats drive tiny buses.  
   **Simpler English:** The illustrator drew a playful, imaginary city where cats drive tiny buses.
2. A whimsical lamp shaped like a mushroom stood beside the bed.  
   **Simpler English:** A playfully unusual mushroom-shaped lamp stood beside the bed.
3. Her whimsical stories turn ordinary objects into unlikely heroes.  
   **Simpler English:** Her playfully imaginative stories make ordinary objects into unexpected heroes.

**Word family:** whimsically *(adverb)*：俏皮奇趣地；whimsy *(noun)*：奇趣、異想天開的特質；whim *(noun; related)*：一時的奇想。  
**Compare:** **Playful**（俏皮的）is broader and can describe teasing without fantasy. **Eccentric**（古怪而有個性的）emphasizes unusual habits or style. Whimsical often adds a light, imaginative charm; eccentric does not necessarily do so.

### 3. Pretentious — CEFR unverified

**English:** Trying too hard to seem important, cultured, or clever in a way that feels affected.  
**繁體中文：** 裝腔作勢的；故作高深的。  
**Source:** [Cambridge Dictionary](https://dictionary.cambridge.org/dictionary/english/pretentious). Disapproving; it expresses the speaker's judgment.

1. The café's pretentious menu made a simple sandwich sound like a philosophical concept.  
   **Simpler English:** The café's menu tried too hard to sound clever when describing a simple sandwich.
2. His review felt pretentious because it used complicated language without explaining the film.  
   **Simpler English:** His review seemed to be showing off with difficult words instead of explaining the film.
3. The exhibition is ambitious without being pretentious.  
   **Simpler English:** The exhibition aims high without trying too hard to seem important or clever.

**Word family:** pretentiously *(adverb)*：裝腔作勢地；pretentiousness *(noun)*：做作、故作高深。  
**Compare:** **Sophisticated**（精緻成熟的）often praises genuine complexity or polish; pretentious criticizes a perceived attempt to impress. **Pompous**（自負而浮誇的）often concerns self-important speech or behavior. Complex art is not automatically pretentious, and simple art is not automatically sincere.

### 4. Irreverent — CEFR unverified

**English:** Treating respected people, institutions, or traditions with less seriousness or respect than expected, often humorously.  
**繁體中文：** 不拘敬意的；以戲謔態度挑戰權威或傳統的。  
**Source:** [Cambridge Dictionary](https://dictionary.cambridge.org/dictionary/english/irreverent). Can be approving or critical depending on the audience.

1. The comedian gave an irreverent account of royal ceremonies.  
   **Simpler English:** The comedian described royal ceremonies with jokes rather than the usual respect.
2. The magazine is known for its irreverent take on luxury fashion.  
   **Simpler English:** The magazine is known for making fun of luxury fashion instead of treating it seriously.
3. Her irreverent poster turns a formal business slogan into a joke.  
   **Simpler English:** Her poster makes a joke out of a formal business slogan rather than treating it respectfully.

**Word family:** irreverence *(noun)*：不敬、戲謔權威的態度；irreverently *(adverb)*：不拘敬意地；reverent *(adjective; opposite)*：恭敬的。  
**Compare:** **Disrespectful**（不尊重的）usually criticizes a failure to show respect. **Satirical**（諷刺性的）uses humor or irony to expose faults. Irreverent humor may be affectionate rather than hostile, and may be silly without making a developed social criticism.

### 5. Quaint — C2

**English:** Charming because something is unusual and especially old-fashioned.  
**繁體中文：** 古雅有趣的；因老派而別具魅力的。  
**Level verification:** [Cambridge Dictionary](https://dictionary.cambridge.org/dictionary/english/quaint) explicitly labels this attractive-old-fashioned meaning **C2**.

1. We stayed in a quaint cottage beside the canal.  
   **Simpler English:** We stayed in a charming, old-fashioned cottage beside the canal.
2. The shop has a quaint wooden sign painted by hand.  
   **Simpler English:** The shop has a charming, old-fashioned wooden sign painted by hand.
3. I liked the village's quaint tea rooms and narrow lanes.  
   **Simpler English:** I liked the village's charming, old-fashioned tea rooms and narrow lanes.

**Derived forms:** quaintly *(adverb)*：古雅有趣地；quaintness *(noun)*：古雅情趣。  
**Compare:** **Old-fashioned**（老派的）does not necessarily imply charm; quaint usually does in this sense. **Picturesque**（如畫的）emphasizes visual attractiveness, especially scenery. A modern harbor can be picturesque without being quaint. Calling someone's beliefs “quaint” can sound dismissive, so use care outside descriptions of places or objects.

## Two useful phrases — 2 minutes

### Business: Strike a balance

**English:** Find a suitable combination of competing aims or qualities.  
**繁體中文：** 取得適當平衡。  
**Example:** We need to strike a balance between a playful brand voice and clear information.  
**Simpler English:** We need a suitable mix of playful language and clear information.  
**Pattern:** strike a balance between A and B.

### Everyday life: Not my cup of tea

**English:** Not something I particularly like or enjoy.  
**繁體中文：** 不是我的菜；不合我的喜好。  
**Example:** The café is popular, but its theatrical décor is not my cup of tea.  
**Simpler English:** The café is popular, but I don't particularly like its theatrical décor.  
**Tone:** Informal and subjective; it expresses your preference without declaring something objectively bad.

## Reading — 3 minutes

### Finding the right kind of character

*Original fictional article.*

Mina was helping a small bookshop redesign its reading room. The owner wanted visitors to feel welcome, but the first proposal looked like an expensive hotel lobby. Mina suggested a more **understated** approach: comfortable chairs, warm lamps, and shelves that let the books attract attention.

The building already had a **quaint** staircase with a curved wooden rail. Rather than hide its age, the team repaired it and kept the small brass numbers on each step. These details gave the room character without requiring an invented story about its history.

A local illustrator proposed a **whimsical** mural showing foxes reading newspapers and birds delivering letters. Some staff loved it. Others worried that it would make the room feel designed only for children. Mina asked the team to **strike a balance** by keeping the mural in one corner and leaving the main reading area quiet.

The wording on the entrance sign caused another debate. One draft described the shop as “a sanctuary for the intellectually exceptional.” The owner laughed and called it **pretentious**. They replaced it with a simple invitation: “Come in, find a book, stay a while.”

For the opening poster, the illustrator added an **irreverent** joke about people who buy impressive books but never read them. It teased literary status without insulting a particular customer. The staff agreed that this suited the shop better than grand claims about intelligence.

At the opening, a visitor smiled and said, “Animal murals are **not my cup of tea**—not usually, anyway.” She sat beside the fox painting with a novel. Mina was pleased. The room did not need to satisfy every taste perfectly; it needed a clear personality and enough space for different people to enjoy it.

**理解檢查：** Why did the team reject the first entrance sign? How did it balance different preferences about the mural?  
**繁體中文提示：** 留意故作高深的文字，以及把俏皮壁畫集中在一角的折衷做法。
