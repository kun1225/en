## Vocabulary — 7 minutes

Read each example aloud. Comparisons describe common tendencies, not absolute boundaries. CEFR ratings apply to the stated target meanings, not automatically to their word families. Definitions and examples are original.

### 1. Feasible — C1 · adjective

**English:** Possible to carry out successfully under the actual conditions.  
**繁體中文：** 可實行的；在現有條件下做得到的。  
**Verification:** [Cambridge Dictionary](https://dictionary.cambridge.org/us/dictionary/english/feasible) marks the possible-to-do meaning **C1**.

1. A four-day schedule is feasible if we reduce the number of meetings.  
   **Simpler English:** We can make a four-day schedule work if we reduce the number of meetings.
2. The studio needs to check whether home delivery is financially feasible.  
   **Simpler English:** The studio needs to check whether it can afford to offer home delivery.
3. It isn't feasible to interview every customer before Friday.  
   **Simpler English:** We can't realistically interview every customer before Friday.

**Word family:** feasibility *(noun)*：可行性；feasibly *(adverb)*：在可行的情況下（較少用）；infeasible *(adjective)*：不可行的。  
**Compare two words:** **Possible**（可能的）is broader: something may be possible in theory but not feasible with your resources. **Practical**（實際可用的）often stresses convenience or usefulness; feasible focuses on whether you can carry it out. These meanings overlap.  
**Pattern:** feasible to do something; a feasibility study.

### 2. Lucrative — C2 · adjective

**English:** Bringing in a large amount of money or profit.  
**繁體中文：** 賺錢的；獲利豐厚的。  
**Verification:** [Cambridge Dictionary](https://dictionary.cambridge.org/dictionary/english/lucrative) labels the money-producing meaning **C2**.

1. She turned a weekend photography hobby into a lucrative business.  
   **Simpler English:** She turned taking photos as a weekend hobby into a business that made a lot of money.
2. The agency won a lucrative contract with a hotel group.  
   **Simpler English:** The agency won a contract with a hotel group that would bring in a lot of money.
3. A lucrative job may still be a poor fit for your lifestyle.  
   **Simpler English:** A job that pays a lot may still not suit the way you live.

**Derived form:** lucratively *(adverb)*：獲利豐厚地。The adjective is much more common; a phrase such as “a lucrative business” usually sounds more natural than an adverb construction.  
**Compare two words:** **Profitable**（有利潤的）can mean simply earning more than the costs; lucrative often suggests substantial earnings. **Rewarding**（有成就感的；有回報的）often concerns satisfaction, though it can include money. A rewarding role need not be lucrative.  
**Pattern:** a lucrative contract / career / market.

### 3. Reluctant — C1 · adjective

**English:** Not wanting to do something, often causing hesitation or delay.  
**繁體中文：** 不情願的；勉強的。  
**Verification:** [Cambridge Dictionary](https://dictionary.cambridge.org/dictionary/english/reluctant?topic=unwilling-and-reluctant) labels the unwilling-to-act meaning **C1**.

1. He was reluctant to accept a role that required frequent travel.  
   **Simpler English:** He didn't really want to accept a job that required him to travel often.
2. I'm reluctant to promise a deadline before seeing the brief.  
   **Simpler English:** I don't really want to promise a deadline before reading the project instructions.
3. The owner was reluctant to raise prices for regular customers.  
   **Simpler English:** The owner didn't really want to raise prices for regular customers.

**Word family:** reluctance *(noun)*：不情願；reluctantly *(adverb)*：勉強地、不情願地。  
**Compare two words:** **Hesitant**（猶豫的）often emphasizes uncertainty or a lack of confidence; reluctant emphasizes not wanting to act. **Unwilling**（不願意的）can sound firmer, while reluctant often allows for eventual agreement. Neither word guarantees what the person will finally do.  
**Pattern:** reluctant to + verb; reluctance to + verb.

### 4. Incentive — C1 · noun, countable or uncountable

**English:** Something that encourages someone to act or make an effort.  
**繁體中文：** 誘因；激勵因素；獎勵措施。  
**Verification:** [Oxford's CEFR wordlist](https://www.oxfordlearnersdictionaries.com/wordlists/oxford3000-5000) lists incentive *(noun)* as **C1**. [Cambridge's entry](https://dictionary.cambridge.org/dictionary/english/incentive) supports the encouragement meaning used here. Oxford's rating is for the word and part of speech rather than a separate rating of every sense.

1. Flexible hours are a strong incentive to stay with the company.  
   **Simpler English:** Flexible hours give people a strong reason to stay with the company.
2. The shop offers a discount as an incentive to bring reusable cups.  
   **Simpler English:** The shop offers a discount to encourage people to bring cups they can use again.
3. Without a clear benefit, customers have little incentive to switch services.  
   **Simpler English:** Without a clear benefit, customers have little reason to change services.

**Word family:** incentivize / incentivise *(verb)*：以誘因鼓勵；disincentive *(noun)*：降低意願的因素、阻力。  
**Compare two words:** **Motivation**（動機、動力）often describes someone's reasons or drive; an incentive is commonly a condition or benefit encouraging action. **Reward**（獎勵）is something received for an action; an expected reward can act as an incentive beforehand. Incentives need not be financial.  
**Pattern:** an incentive to do something; an incentive for someone.

### 5. Tedious — C1 · adjective

**English:** Boring and tiring because something takes too long or involves repeated actions.  
**繁體中文：** 冗長乏味的；單調累人的。  
**Verification:** [Cambridge Dictionary](https://dictionary.cambridge.org/dictionary/english/tedious) marks this boredom-causing meaning **C1**.

1. Copying every receipt into a spreadsheet is tedious.  
   **Simpler English:** Copying every receipt into a spreadsheet is boring and tiring.
2. The instructions turned a simple task into a tedious process.  
   **Simpler English:** The instructions made a simple task take too long and become boring.
3. We automated the most tedious part of preparing the report.  
   **Simpler English:** We made the most boring and time-consuming part of preparing the report run automatically.

**Word family:** tediously *(adverb)*：冗長乏味地；tedium *(noun)*：單調乏味、沉悶。  
**Compare two words:** **Boring**（無聊的）is the broader everyday word for lacking interest. **Repetitive**（重複的）describes repeated actions or content, which may or may not be unpleasant. Tedious usually adds a negative judgment about the time and effort involved.  
**Pattern:** a tedious task / process; find something tedious.

## Two useful phrases — 2 minutes

### Business: Draw the line at

**English:** Set a limit by saying that a particular action or condition is unacceptable.  
**繁體中文：** 以……為底線；拒絕接受……。  
**Example:** We can accept occasional evening calls, but we draw the line at being available every weekend.  
**Simpler English:** We can accept occasional evening calls, but we won't agree to be available every weekend.  
**Usage:** draw the line at + noun or -ing. Direct and firm; explain your reason during a negotiation.

### Everyday life: Sleep on it

**English:** Wait until the next day before deciding, giving yourself time to think.  
**繁體中文：** 先考慮一晚；明天再決定。  
**Example:** I'm interested in the apartment, but I'd like to sleep on it before making an offer.  
**Simpler English:** I'm interested in the apartment, but I'd like to wait until tomorrow to decide before making an offer.  
**Usage:** Conversational; the “it” refers to a decision already under discussion.

## Reading — 3 minutes

### A better offer?

*Original fictional article.*

When Nora received an offer to manage a small design studio, her first reaction was excitement. The salary was higher than anything she had earned before, and the role could lead to **lucrative** consulting projects. Yet the contract required her to answer client messages during evenings and weekends. She was **reluctant** to accept before understanding what that would mean for her family.

Instead of replying immediately, Nora asked to **sleep on it**. That evening, she listed what she enjoyed about her current job: focused mornings, time for exercise, and the freedom to collect her daughter from school. A bigger salary was attractive, but it was not her only reason for working.

The following day, she spoke with the studio owner. Nora explained that a flexible schedule would be a stronger **incentive** than a small additional bonus. She could handle an occasional urgent request, but she would **draw the line at** routine weekend availability.

The owner listened. He admitted that several clients sent late messages simply because nobody had explained when to expect a response. Together, they discussed whether a shared support calendar was **feasible**. Another manager could cover urgent requests on alternate weeks, while ordinary questions would wait until morning.

Nora also asked about the work itself. Much of the reporting sounded **tedious**, involving the same figures copied into several documents. The owner agreed that she could simplify that process during her first month.

She accepted a revised offer a few days later. The salary had not changed, but the expectations had. By discussing the details, Nora found a role that offered both professional growth and a daily routine she could maintain.

**理解檢查：** What mattered to Nora besides salary? Which two changes helped her accept the role?  
**繁體中文提示：** 留意彈性工時、週末界線，以及簡化重複報表的安排。
