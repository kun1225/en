## Vocabulary — 7 minutes

Definitions, comparisons, and examples below are original. CEFR labels apply to the indicated meanings; derived forms are not assigned the same level automatically. Comparisons describe tendencies, with overlapping uses.

### 1. Tangible — C2 · adjective

**Definition:** Real enough to be clearly noticed, demonstrated, or experienced.  
**繁體中文：** 實在的；可明確感受到或看見的。  
**Level verification:** [Cambridge: tangible](https://dictionary.cambridge.org/dictionary/english/tangible) labels the real, demonstrable or experienceable meaning **C2**.

1. The new booking system brought tangible benefits to the volunteers.
2. We need tangible evidence that the training is helping.
3. Finishing the first chapter gave me a tangible sense of progress.

**Word family:** tangibly (adverb)：明顯可感地；intangible (adjective)：無形的、難以具體衡量的。  
**Two related words:** **concrete**（具體的）often emphasizes specific details rather than general ideas; **measurable**（可衡量的）emphasizes something that can be assessed by a quantity or scale. Tangible stresses a real, noticeable result; a benefit can feel tangible without being easy to measure.  
**Useful pattern:** tangible benefits / results / evidence.

### 2. Ingenious — C1 · adjective

**Definition:** Showing clever skill in finding an original solution or designing something.  
**繁體中文：** 巧妙的；別出心裁的。  
**Level verification:** [Oxford: ingenious](https://www.oxfordlearnersdictionaries.com/us/definition/english/ingenious) marks this clever invention or problem-solving meaning **C1** under Personal qualities.

1. She found an ingenious way to turn old shelves into display stands.
2. The engineer designed an ingenious lock that needs no electricity.
3. His ingenious solution saved us hours of manual work.

**Word family:** ingeniously (adverb)：巧妙地；ingenuity (uncountable noun)：巧思、獨創能力。  
**Two related words:** **clever**（聰明的、巧妙的）is broader and more conversational; **inventive**（富創意的）often emphasizes producing fresh ideas. Ingenious commonly praises how cleverly a particular solution works; inventive often describes someone's continuing ability, though both can describe designs.  
**Useful pattern:** an ingenious solution / device / way to do something.

### 3. Detrimental — C2 · adjective · formal

**Definition:** Having an effect that harms something or makes it worse.  
**繁體中文：** 有害的；不利的。  
**Level verification:** [Cambridge: detrimental](https://dictionary.cambridge.org/dictionary/english/detrimental) labels the harm-causing meaning **C2** and marks it formal.

1. Constant interruptions can be detrimental to concentration.
2. The rushed decision had a detrimental effect on staff morale.
3. Cutting maintenance costs could be detrimental to the building's condition.

**Word family:** detriment (noun)：損害、不利；detrimentally (adverb)：有害地、不利地（較正式）。  
**Two related words:** **harmful**（有害的）is the more general everyday choice; **counterproductive**（適得其反的）emphasizes working against the intended goal. A policy can be detrimental to morale and counterproductive if that damage also prevents its intended improvement in performance.  
**Useful pattern:** detrimental to + noun; a detrimental effect on + noun.

### 4. Inevitable — C1 · adjective

**Definition:** Certain to happen, with no way to stop or avoid it.  
**繁體中文：** 不可避免的；必然發生的。  
**Level verification:** [Cambridge: inevitable](https://dictionary.cambridge.org/dictionary/english/inevitable) labels this unavoidable-event meaning **C1**.

1. Some wear is inevitable when equipment is used every day.
2. With only one seat left and two passengers waiting, disappointment was inevitable.
3. Change is inevitable, but we can choose how to respond.

**Word family:** inevitably (adverb)：不可避免地、必然地；inevitability (noun)：不可避免性。  
**Two related words:** **unavoidable**（無法避免的）is often interchangeable, with a practical emphasis on having no alternative; **likely**（很可能的）expresses probability rather than certainty. Calling a delay inevitable is a stronger claim than calling it likely.  
**Useful pattern:** an inevitable consequence; it is inevitable that + clause.

### 5. Imaginative — C1 · adjective

**Definition:** Using fresh ideas to create something original or interesting.  
**繁體中文：** 富有想像力的；有創意的。  
**Level verification:** [Cambridge: imaginative](https://dictionary.cambridge.org/dictionary/english/imaginative?topic=intelligence) labels the new, original, clever meaning **C1**.

1. The exhibition offers an imaginative approach to local history.
2. Her imaginative storytelling kept the children interested.
3. We need an imaginative campaign that makes people curious.

**Word family:** imagination (noun)：想像力；imaginatively (adverb)：富有想像力地；unimaginative (adjective)：缺乏創意的。  
**Two related words:** **creative**（有創造力的）is a broad word for producing new ideas or work; **imaginary**（想像出來的、虛構的）describes something that does not actually exist. An imaginative designer may create a real playground inspired by an imaginary world.  
**Useful pattern:** an imaginative approach / design / use of space.

## Two phrases — 2 minutes

### Business: Take stock of

**Explanation:** Pause to review a situation, resources, or progress before deciding what to do next.  
**繁體中文：** 盤點；全面檢視目前的情況。  
**Example:** Before we request more funding, let's take stock of what the pilot has achieved.  
**Usage:** take stock of + noun; professional but also natural in everyday reflection.

### Everyday life: Call it a day

**Explanation:** Decide to stop an activity for now because you have done enough.  
**繁體中文：** 今天就做到這裡；收工。  
**Example:** We've cleaned the kitchen and the balcony, so let's call it a day.  
**Usage:** Informal; useful for work, study, or household tasks.

## Reading — 3 minutes

### A repair café worth returning to

*Original fictional article.*

When Mei suggested turning an empty shop into a weekend repair café, her neighbors liked the idea. They imagined a place where people could fix lamps, exchange skills, and meet over coffee. Yet enthusiasm alone would not pay the rent. Before signing a lease, Mei asked the group to **take stock of** its resources: six volunteers, borrowed tools, and enough money for a short trial.

An **imaginative** designer proposed arranging the room like a small neighborhood street, with separate corners for different repairs. Another volunteer offered an **ingenious** solution to the shortage of tables: old doors mounted securely on folding supports. Both ideas helped the space feel welcoming without requiring expensive furniture.

On the first Saturday, visitors arrived with broken appliances and high expectations. Some disappointment was **inevitable** because the volunteers could not repair every item. However, Mei worried that promising too much would be **detrimental** to trust. She put a sign near the entrance explaining what the team could safely attempt and when specialist help would be necessary.

By evening, the project had produced **tangible** results: eight repaired lamps, three working fans, and several visitors eager to return. The volunteers were tired, so Mei suggested they **call it a day** rather than accept another complicated repair.

The next morning, they reviewed the visitors' comments. People valued the conversations as much as the repairs. Mei realized that success meant more than counting fixed objects. The café also gave neighbors a reason to help one another. For the next session, the team planned fewer appointments and more time for teaching basic skills.

**理解檢查：** Why did Mei explain the café's limits? What value did visitors find beyond repaired objects?  
**繁體中文提示：** 誠實說明能力範圍可以保護信任；修理之外，鄰里交流與互相學習也有價值。
