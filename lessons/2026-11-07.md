## Vocabulary · 7 minutes

Read the examples aloud. The comparisons below describe common tendencies; related words can overlap. CEFR labels apply to the target meanings, not automatically to every word-family member.

### 1. Resourceful — C2 · adjective

**English:** Good at finding ways to solve problems using what is available.  
**繁體中文：** 足智多謀的；善於運用現有資源解決問題的。  
**Level verification:** [Cambridge Dictionary](https://dictionary.cambridge.org/dictionary/english/resourceful) marks the problem-solving meaning **C2**; approving in tone.

1. Our resourceful coordinator found a free venue when the original booking fell through.
2. You need to be resourceful when travelling with a limited budget.
3. The resourceful technician used a spare cable to restore the connection.

**Derived forms:** resourcefulness *(noun, uncountable)* — 應變能力；足智多謀。resourcefully *(adverb)* — 機靈地；善用資源地。

**Compare:**

- **Inventive** — 富有創意的：emphasizes creating new ideas or methods. A resourceful person may simply reuse an existing tool cleverly.
- **Adaptable** — 能適應變化的：emphasizes adjusting to new conditions. Resourceful puts more emphasis on finding a solution, often with limited resources.

### 2. Indispensable — C2 · adjective

**English:** So important or useful that you cannot manage properly without it or them.  
**繁體中文：** 不可或缺的；必不可少的。  
**Level verification:** [Cambridge Dictionary](https://dictionary.cambridge.org/dictionary/english/indispensable) marks this necessity meaning **C2**.

1. A reliable contact list is indispensable when an event changes at short notice.
2. Her local knowledge proved indispensable to the visiting team.
3. My calendar has become indispensable for keeping track of family commitments.

**Derived forms:** indispensability *(noun, uncountable)* — 不可或缺性（較正式）。dispensable *(adjective; opposite formed by removing the negative prefix)* — 可有可無的；非必要的。

**Compare:**

- **Essential** — 必要的：often interchangeable with indispensable. Indispensable can give stronger emphasis to being unable to manage without a particular person or thing.
- **Invaluable** — 極有價值的：stresses exceptional usefulness or value; it does not necessarily mean something is required. Invaluable does **not** mean worthless.

### 3. Detrimental — C2 · adjective

**English:** Having an effect that harms someone or something.  
**繁體中文：** 有害的；不利的。  
**Level verification:** [Cambridge Dictionary](https://dictionary.cambridge.org/us/dictionary/english/detrimental) marks the harmful-effect meaning **C2** and labels it formal.

1. Repeated last-minute changes can be detrimental to staff morale.
2. Skipping every lunch break had a detrimental effect on her concentration.
3. The committee rejected a policy it considered detrimental to small businesses.

**Word family:** detriment *(noun; base)* — 損害；不利，常見於 *to the detriment of*。detrimentally *(adverb; derived)* — 有害地；不利地（正式）。

**Compare:**

- **Harmful** — 有害的：the broader, more everyday choice, especially for health or physical harm. Detrimental is common in formal discussion of effects on progress, interests, or well-being.
- **Counterproductive** — 適得其反的：specifically means producing the opposite of the intended result. A detrimental action causes harm without necessarily reversing its intended goal.

**Pattern:** detrimental **to** something; a detrimental effect **on** something.

### 4. Unanimous — C2 · adjective

**English:** Agreed to or supported by every member of a group.  
**繁體中文：** 全體一致的；無異議的。  
**Level verification:** [Cambridge Dictionary](https://dictionary.cambridge.org/dictionary/english/unanimous) marks this complete-agreement meaning **C2**.

1. The board gave unanimous approval to the revised budget.
2. We were unanimous in our decision to move the workshop indoors.
3. The judges reached a unanimous verdict after reviewing the evidence.

**Derived forms:** unanimously *(adverb)* — 全體一致地。unanimity *(noun, uncountable)* — 全體一致；無異議（正式）。

**Compare:**

- **Widespread** — 廣泛的：widespread support comes from many people, but some may disagree. Unanimous support includes everyone in the stated group.
- **United** — 團結的；立場一致的：often stresses solidarity or a shared purpose. A united team can still debate details; unanimous identifies complete agreement on a particular matter.

### 5. Inevitable — C1 · adjective

**English:** Certain to occur and impossible to prevent.  
**繁體中文：** 不可避免的；必然發生的。  
**Level verification:** [Cambridge Dictionary](https://dictionary.cambridge.org/us/dictionary/english/inevitable) marks this certainty-and-unavoidability meaning **C1**.

1. With the only access road closed, a delay was inevitable.
2. Some disagreement is inevitable when a large group makes decisions.
3. Failure is not inevitable if we change our approach now.

**Derived forms:** inevitably *(adverb)* — 不可避免地；必然地。inevitability *(noun)* — 必然性；不可避免的事。

**Compare:**

- **Unavoidable** — 無法避免的：very close in meaning; often emphasizes the inability to prevent a particular problem or obligation. Inevitable often presents an outcome as the natural result of circumstances.
- **Likely** — 很可能的：expresses probability, not certainty. Calling a delay inevitable makes a much stronger claim than calling it likely.

## Two useful phrases · 2 minutes

### Business: Keep someone in the loop

**English:** Continue giving someone the information they need about developments.  
**繁體中文：** 讓某人持續掌握最新進度；隨時告知相關消息。  
**Example:** Please keep the client in the loop about any changes to the delivery date.

**Pattern and tone:** keep + person + in the loop; conversational, common in workplace emails. Name who needs updates.

### Everyday: Play it by ear

**English:** Decide what to do as a situation develops rather than fixing every detail beforehand.  
**繁體中文：** 見機行事；到時候再看情況決定。  
**Example:** We haven't booked dinner; we'll play it by ear after the hike.

**Tone:** Informal. Useful for flexible social plans; avoid using it to dismiss a commitment that someone needs you to confirm.

## Reading · 3 minutes

### A different kind of successful weekend

*Original fictional article.*

On Friday afternoon, Mei learned that heavy rain would reach the town before its weekend repair fair. The fair usually filled an outdoor square with neighbours fixing bicycles, lamps, and kitchen tools. With the square already closed by the council, a change of venue was **inevitable**. Cancelling, however, was still a choice.

Mei called the volunteers. One **resourceful** member suggested using the community library after its morning programme. Another offered folding tables from a nearby school. Within an hour, they had a smaller space and enough equipment for half the original number of repair stations.

Before confirming the move, Mei asked whether fewer stations would mean longer queues. Volunteers warned that crowded entrances could be **detrimental** to visitors' comfort and make the library difficult to use. They proposed timed entry and a separate collection desk. The library manager's knowledge of the building was **indispensable**: she identified a side door that could keep arriving visitors away from people collecting repaired items.

The team was **unanimous** in supporting the revised plan. Mei promised to **keep everyone in the loop** through a short message that evening, followed by a notice at the original venue. Each update would include the address, entry times, and a phone number.

After the fair, the volunteers had hoped to share dinner outdoors. Nobody wanted another detailed planning meeting, so they agreed to **play it by ear**. If the rain stopped, they would visit the night market; otherwise, they would find a nearby café.

The fair repaired fewer objects than expected, but visitors left satisfied. For Mei, success meant protecting the purpose of the event while changing how it happened.

**Quick reflection:** What made the library manager indispensable? Which plan required firm details, and which could remain flexible?
